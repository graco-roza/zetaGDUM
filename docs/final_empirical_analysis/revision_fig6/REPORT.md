# Redesigned Figure 6: what MS-GDUM adds beyond spread-only MS-GDM and total dissimilarity

Branch `final-empirical-analysis`; analysis scripts in this directory (`run_all.sh`; run from `final/` with `ZG_LIB6` = the **principal additive package**, branch `principal/additive-msgdum`; see `../revision_pkg`). No explicit interactions appear in the model, figure or narrative. EPA shared lake pool (1,077 lakes; principal), full pools as sensitivity; macroinvertebrate orders 2 and 5, zooplankton orders 2 and 4. All numbers come from `tables/`. Descriptive model predictions are not causal effects.

## Design
* **Models.** Spread-only MS-GDM and additive MS-GDUM, both fitted with the coherent component architecture (`ugdm_fit_components`; total dissimilarity and the heterogeneity share, occupancy-associated = total - heterogeneity) on identical 6,000 training combinations, response definitions, I-spline bases, link function, nonnegativity constraints and ridge penalty (0.001). They differ only by the TP, TN and Chl-a position terms (geographic distance is in both).
* **Matched configurations.** The earlier support rules are unchanged (reference configurations drawn from observed combinations; TN, Chl-a, location and the internal arrangement of each TP profile preserved; TP positions 10, 35, 100 ug/L; >= 10 common profiles, >= 3 grid points, >= 0.20 log10 spread width). Matched spread interval: macroinvertebrates 0.45-0.70, zooplankton 0.20-0.65 (log10 units).
* **Contrast A:** TP spread increases (lower to upper end of the matched interval) at each TP position. **Contrast B:** TP position increases (Low to High, also Low to Intermediate and Intermediate to High) at fixed spread (interval lower end, midpoint, upper end), shown at the midpoint. For the additive model the link-scale change is decomposed into **spline-slope terms** (the TP spread terms, evaluated at the new position; spread-only models have only these) and **explicit position terms**; the response-scale change is split between them by a two-factor Shapley decomposition (`fig6_position_decomposition.csv`, `FigureS6_position_decomposition`). Differences between TP positions in the spread-only model are therefore not attributed to position terms.
* **Compensation.** gross = |dO| + |dH|, net = |dT|, compensated = (gross - net)/2 (equals min(|dO|, |dH|) when the signs oppose; 0 when they agree), all in percentage points. The fraction compensated is reported only where gross >= 1 pp, never as a ratio near zero.

## Environmental support (`fig6_support_summary.csv`)
Shared pool: **622** supported reference configurations at macroinvertebrate k = 2 but only **12** at k = 5; **545** at zooplankton k = 2 and **14** at k = 4 (246 common spread intervals at k = 2 for both taxa, 4 at macroinvertebrate k = 5 and 38 at zooplankton k = 4). Full pools: 531 / **0** (macroinvertebrate k = 5 has no common support and is not drawn) and 435 / 13. Higher-order conditional contrasts therefore rest on a handful of reference configurations. Across the 30 random splits the matched interval is supported by the training sites in 30/30 splits at k = 2 for both taxa, but in only **3/30** (macroinvertebrate k = 5) and **7/30** (zooplankton k = 4) splits of the shared pool (0/30 and 14/30 in the full pool) (`fig6_summary_split_support.csv`).

## Results
**1. Does environmental position add information beyond spread?** Yes in held-out prediction, with caveats.
* Held-out D2 (30 random site-level splits, shared pool; additive minus spread-only, median [95%], positive splits): total dissimilarity +0.015 [0.004, 0.024] 29/30 (macroinvertebrate k = 2), +0.032 [0.020, 0.052] 30/30 (k = 5), +0.004 [-0.004, 0.011] 24/30 (zooplankton k = 2; not distinguishable from zero), +0.017 [0.004, 0.024] 29/30 (k = 4). Occupancy-associated: +0.011, +0.013, +0.010, +0.022; heterogeneity: +0.005 [-0.011, 0.012] 23/30, +0.010, +0.010, +0.021 (`fig6_summary_heldout_D2.csv`).
* Predicted contrasts. TP position matters mostly through the explicit position terms: Low to High position at fixed mid spread changes total dissimilarity by +6.9 pp (macroinvertebrate k = 2), +1.0 pp (k = 5), +1.9 pp (zooplankton k = 2) and +0.25 pp (k = 4) in the additive model, but by +0.77, +0.01, +0.15 and -0.001 pp in the spread-only model, which can only express position dependence through nonlinear I-splines. In the additive model the spline-slope part of that change is -0.13, -0.005, +0.08 and -0.001 pp, i.e. essentially all (>= 100%) of the position response is carried by the explicit terms; the spread-only response arises from refitted spread curves.
* Contrast A (spread) is small and similar between models: dT = +0.03 to +0.72 pp across positions. The additive model changes how the spread effect varies with position (e.g. macroinvertebrate k = 2: +0.59, +0.46, +0.35 pp at Low/Intermediate/High versus +0.39, +0.58, +0.72 pp in the spread-only model), so the *direction* of the dependence on position differs between the models; both are descriptive.
* Negative/inconclusive: for zooplankton k = 2 the held-out gain for total dissimilarity is not clearly positive; position contrasts at k = 4 and 5 rest on 12-14 configurations and 3-7 splits.

**2. Can opposing occupancy and heterogeneity responses conceal responses in total dissimilarity?** Yes, descriptively, in both contrasts and in both models.
* Contrast A: occupancy-associated dissimilarity increases and abundance heterogeneity decreases as TP spread increases at every position, order and model (all-site: dO = +0.35 to +1.15 pp, dH = -0.27 to -1.08 pp); signs oppose in 93-100% of splits (shared pool; 86% in one zooplankton k = 4 spread-only cell), median compensated change 0.13-1.09 pp, 26-49% of the gross change where gross >= 1 pp. The net total change is 17-39% of the gross component change at k = 2 and only 3-4% at the higher orders (macroinvertebrate k = 5: 0.04-0.07 pp against 1.0-2.2 pp; zooplankton k = 4: 0.03-0.04 pp against 0.7-1.5 pp), so a total-dissimilarity model would show almost no spread response at the higher orders although both components change by about 0.3-1.2 pp.
* Contrast B (additive): Low to High position gives dO = +9.4, +8.1, +6.5 and +5.9 pp and dH = -2.5, -7.1, -4.6 and -5.7 pp, net total +6.9, +1.0, +1.9 and +0.25 pp (compensated 2.5-7.1 pp; fraction 0.21-0.49). The spread-only models give <= 0.9 pp in every component. Across splits the additive position contrast has opposing component signs in 100% of splits (k = 2: dO +9.0, dH -3.1 pp (median); macroinvertebrate k = 5: +9.3, -8.1 pp).
* Whether compensation is consistent: it is consistent in sign across models, positions and splits (above), and its size depends on the model (position terms change the size at fixed spread) and on order (larger share compensated at the higher orders). The independent marginal fits of Version B show the same opposite-signed explicit position contributions for occupancy-associated and heterogeneity components; the coherent and independent fits are different analyses and are labelled as such.
* Limits: compensation is partly built into the decomposition (total = occupancy + heterogeneity); a negative heterogeneity change with a positive occupancy change is a description of the components, not evidence of two causes. Zooplankton k = 4 split intervals for dH span zero at all three positions (additive; e.g. [-0.40, +0.09] pp at Intermediate), based on 7 splits.

## Robustness
Split-to-split variability of the contrasts is in `fig6_summary_splits_contrast_A.csv` and `_B.csv` (median and 95% interval over supported splits). At k = 2 intervals for dO and dH exclude zero for macroinvertebrates (dO +0.19 to +1.6 pp) but include zero for zooplankton in several cells (additive High dH [-0.86, +0.17] pp). Full-pool results (`*_full_pool` figures; `pool == "full"` rows) reproduce the signs for the supported panels: dO +0.18 to +1.16 pp and dH -0.16 to -0.68 pp for the spread contrast; Low to High position change at mid spread dT +5.9, +1.6 and +0.39 pp (additive; spread-only 0.76, 0.08, -0.0005 pp) with dO +10.5, +3.1, +4.9 pp and dH -4.6, -1.6, -4.5 pp; macroinvertebrate k = 5 is unavailable.

## Other corrections
* **Figure 5 matched penalties.** The earlier spread-only comparator was an unpenalised NNLS fit; it is now fitted with the same bases, link, constraints and penalties as the position models (`ugdm_fit_position(spread_only = TRUE)`). Consequence: the position gains at orders >= 8 that were negative are positive (e.g. macroinvertebrate relative abundance +0.030 at k = 8, 30/30 splits); the earlier negative findings were an artefact of the unmatched comparator (`../revision_random/REPORT.md`, correction note; tables `rv5m_*`, figures `Figure5_random_validation_matched.*`). The spatial-block supplement was not regenerated.
* **R2 = zeta_2/zeta_1** is included in the retention diagnostics (`../revision_random/tables/order_diagnostics_ecological.csv`): incidence 0.365 (macroinvertebrates, shared pool), 0.272 (zooplankton), 0.498 (BCI); relative abundance 0.178, 0.111, 0.469.

## Limitations
* Higher-order contrasts (macroinvertebrate k = 5, zooplankton k = 4) rest on 12-14 reference configurations and 3-7 of 30 splits; their split-to-split intervals are indicative only.
* One log10-TP position grid (10, 35, 100 ug/L) and one matched spread interval per taxon; the spread interval is short (0.45-0.70 and 0.20-0.65 log10 units), so spread contrasts are small.
* Coherent predictions are model-based; the position terms of TP, TN and Chl-a are collinear (VIF about 4), so TP-specific position effects are not separated from correlated nutrient gradients.
* No analysis was selected on the size or sign of an ecological effect; negative and inconclusive cells are retained in the tables.
