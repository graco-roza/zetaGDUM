# Revised final empirical analyses: random site-level validation, model equivalence, Figure 6 versions, order-selection diagnostics

Branch `final-empirical-analysis`. zetaGDUM commit f7e115f (unmodified; built into a separate library). Communities: BCI trees (1985), EPA NLA 2017 macroinvertebrates and zooplankton (shared 1,077-lake pool principal; full pools sensitivity). No Distance Decay 2.0 data. No manuscript text. Taxa, not species. All numbers below are read from the CSV files in `tables/`; scripts are in this directory (run order in `run_all.sh`, from `final/` with `ZG_LIB6` set). Previous spatial-block results are preserved unchanged (`../fig5_revision`, `../fig6_interaction`) and the former Figure 5 is retained as `figures/FigureS5_spatial_block_validation.*`.

## 1. Random site-level validation (`70`, `71`, `81`)
* 30 independent random 70/30 site splits (`tables/random_splits.csv`, seeds 910001-910030). Shared pool: 754 training / 323 test lakes (identical for both taxa); full pools 757/324 (macroinvertebrates) and 765/328 (zooplankton). Training and evaluation set combinations are drawn only from the respective site pools (3,000 sets each; seeds depend on split and order only), so no site is in both; identical splits and sets are used by every model, order and response representation.
* Environmental centring, scaling and I-spline bases are fitted on the training design; the test design is built with `ref =` the training design. Held-out D2 is against the training mean.
* Summaries are the median and the central 95% interval (2.5-97.5% quantiles) across splits. They describe split-to-split variability; the splits share sites, so they are not independent and the intervals are **not** significance tests. Negative values are retained.

## 2. Figure 5 (`figures/Figure5_random_validation.*`, full pool `FigureS5_random_validation_full_pool.*`, all-split contrasts `FigureS5_random_panelA_splits_*`)
Same design as the previous Figure 5. Panels A and B now use the random splits; panels C and D (conditional contrasts of all-site fits) contain no validation and are unchanged.
* Convergence: 1,440/1,440 fits converged per pool (shared pool: 1 fit with a package warning); no failed fits. Shapley/Owen allocations are missing for a few split/order/response cells in the shared pool (29 of 30 splits at some cells, flagged in `n_splits`); all 30 in the full pool.
* Paired held-out gain of nonlinear position over spread-only, median [95% interval], splits positive (shared pool; `rv5_paired_summary_shared.csv`):
  macroinvertebrate incidence 0.034 [0.007, 0.053] 30/30 at k = 2, 0.015 [-0.006, 0.040] 28/30 at k = 5, -0.001 at k = 10 (14/30) and -0.018 at k = 12 (2/30);
  macroinvertebrate relative abundance 0.013 at k = 2, 0.011 at k = 5, **-0.017 at k = 8, -0.038 at k = 10 (0/30) and -0.059 at k = 12 (0/30)**;
  zooplankton incidence 0.015-0.020 at k = 2-5 (29-30/30; lower limits near 0);
  zooplankton relative abundance 0.001-0.010 with intervals spanning zero (18-24/30 positive).
  The linear-position gains are similar (`rv5_paired_summary_*`). Position therefore adds held-out information at low orders for incidence and for macroinvertebrate relative abundance, but this gain declines with order and reverses to loss at k >= 8 for macroinvertebrate relative abundance, where the spread-only comparator is the better predictor; for zooplankton relative abundance the gain is not distinguishable from zero.
* Held-out D2 (shared; nonlinear position / spread-only): macroinvertebrate incidence 0.209/0.178 (k = 2) to 0.063/0.078 (k = 12); relative abundance 0.109/0.094 to 0.028/0.088; zooplankton incidence 0.121/0.105 to 0.076/0.062 (k = 5); relative abundance 0.039/0.037 to 0.042/0.036.

Zooplankton: distance 0.059; all environmental terms <= 0.021, TP position 0.008 [-0.003, 0.015]. Negative allocations are kept in the tables (`rv5_importance_*`).
* Differences from the spatial validation: gains and importances are estimated on randomly held-out lakes, so spatial autocorrelation is not removed; geographic distance contributes substantially (0.06 of D2). The random-split results should be read as additional information beyond the spread-only model, not as transferability.

## 3. Figure 6 (`figures/figure6_additive/`, `figures/figure6_k2_additive_higher_TP_interaction/`)
* Predictive value of the explicit interaction under random validation (`rv6_paired_summary_*`, coherent component model, held-out total dissimilarity D2, interaction minus additive): TP-only interaction (penalty 1e-6): k = 2 median 0.0000 (BM 15/30 and ZP 10/30 splits positive), largest at macroinvertebrates k = 5 (0.0011 [0.0000, 0.0025], 29/30) and k = 6 (0.0008); zooplankton <= 0.0001. All-predictor interaction: 0.0001-0.0023 (largest at macroinvertebrates k = 4-5). Across the 12 order x taxon cells the median gain of the TP-only interaction is 6.6e-5 (penalty 1e-6), 1.9e-5 (1e-4) and 8e-7 (0.01). In-sample gains are of the same size (macroinvertebrates k = 5: 0.0011). The explicit interaction therefore adds almost no predictive information; at k = 2 it adds none, consistent with the nonlinear additive I-splines already carrying the position dependence.
* Convergence: 2 of 2,520 fits (shared) and 3 of 2,520 (full) did not converge; none removed (flagged).
* Closure: coherent predictions satisfy C + O + H = 1 (C = 1 - total) to 4.7e-17 (maximum closure RMSE over all CV fits; evaluated fraction 1). In the matched-profile predictions occupancy is total minus heterogeneity by construction, so closure there is exact (0). Coherent and independent total-dissimilarity models under identical specifications agree to 1.1e-15 (predictions) and 4.4e-15 (coefficients) (`Figure6_B_fit_checks.csv`).
* Two versions (identical matched profiles, common spread ranges, fixed orders BM 2 and 5, ZP 2 and 4, component definitions): (1) additive at all orders; (2) additive at k = 2 and explicit TP spread-by-position interaction at higher orders (penalty 1e-6). The Figure 6 curves and bars are descriptive all-site fits, as before; random validation was used to evaluate the interaction, not to refit the figure. The full-pool macroinvertebrate k = 5 panel has no common supported contrast (marked unavailable), as in the previous figure. Absolute spline slopes are not compared between orders.

## 4. Model equivalence (`90`, `91`; `figures/FigureS_model_equivalence.*`; `tables/eq_*.csv`)
Numerical implementation check (no cross-validation). EPA shared pool, 3,000 identical sampled combinations per comparison (seed 4242; k = 3 and 5 replay zetadiv's own sampling sequence). Two questions are separated: design agreement and coefficient agreement under the reference's link and criterion. Where the likelihoods or links differ the comparison is labelled **unmatched** and no equivalence is claimed.

| comparison | link / criterion | max abs coefficient difference (BM; ZP) | design agreement |
|---|---|---|---|
| E1 additive MS-GDUM k = 2 (`ugdm_fit`) vs gdmmTMB | both binomial logit; matched | 1.9e-5; 1.8e-4 (intercept <= 3e-6; position <= 1e-5; distance <= 1.1e-5) | basis identical (0); linear predictors differ by <= 1e-4 |
| E2 spread-only k = 2 vs gdm | negative-exponential, gdm deviance; matched (temporary validation fit; independent starts) | 4.9e-6; 2.5e-6 | linear predictor 4e-16 / 1e-15 on the zetaGDUM kernel with gdm's basis; deviance reproduced to 1.5e-9 |
| E3 spread-only k = 3, 5 vs zetadiv | binomial(log), Simpson zeta, sign constraints (`glm.cons`); matched | 1e-15 to 4e-15 | response and set-level design 1e-16 |
| E2n, E3n zetaGDUM native fits vs gdm, zetadiv | logit/dissimilarity vs negative-exponential or log/similarity; **unmatched** | 0.5-1.6 (not claimed equal) | native basis differs |

* Reported per comparison (`eq_coefficients.csv`, `eq_validation_table.csv`): intercept, every I-spline coefficient, summed coefficients per predictor, position coefficients (E1), maximum absolute discrepancy, coefficient correlations (matched: 1.000; unmatched: 0.72-0.94 for all coefficients, 0.66-0.99 for summed splines) and fitted transformation curves (`eq_fitted_curves.csv`).
* Link differences: zetaGDUM uses a fractional logit; gdm uses the negative-exponential link (E2 tested with a temporary routine using gdm's link and deviance, default behaviour unchanged); zetadiv uses a log link on Simpson similarity (E3 used zetadiv's own fitter on the zetaGDUM-built design, so E3 verifies the set-level design and response, not an independent optimiser).
* Basis differences: zetaGDUM's native basis (degree-2 I-splines without interior knot) is identical to gdmmTMB's; gdm and zetadiv use a median knot. The temporary fits therefore use the reference's own basis through zetaGDUM's set-kernel (`mean_pair_isp_cpp`).
* Discrepancies and limits: gdmmTMB reports "singular convergence (7)" with coefficients at its 1e-8 floor, but its predictions and coefficients agree with `ugdm_fit`. gdm's plotted curves (`isplineExtract`) differ from the standard I-spline reconstruction by up to 0.016, although the fitted design reproduces gdm's deviance exactly (the plotting routine is evaluated differently). gdm was compared on environmental predictors only (geographic distance was not matched to gdm's distance basis). gdmmTMB comparison covers the linear-position original structure; the penalised, nonlinear-position and interaction extensions have no reference implementation. Only the shared pool and one sampled combination set per comparison were tested.

## 5. Order selection: the three axes (`95`, `96`; `figures/FigureS_order_selection_diagnostics.*`; `tables/order_diagnostics_*.csv`)
Descriptive; no order is chosen against predictive performance or effect size. EPA environmental diagnostics are identical for the two taxa in the shared pool (same lakes); BCI has habitat classes only (`order_diagnostics_bci_habitat.csv`), no continuous environmental predictors.
* Ecological representation: incidence retention per order rises with order (macroinvertebrates 0.51 at k = 3, 0.63 at k = 5, 0.71 at k = 12); contribution-weighted similarity occupancy rises from 0.51 (k = 2) to 0.72 (k = 12), and the dissimilarity share of the 21 taxa occupying >= 50% of lakes from 0.20 to 0.34; effective shared taxa fall from 47 to 8.5.
* Compositional information: sharing probability and response variance decline together with a rising saturated fraction (macroinvertebrates: P(share >= 1 taxon) 0.99 at k = 2, 0.82 at k = 5, 0.16 at k = 12; component-total variance 1.4e-2 to 3.5e-6; proportion saturated 0.006, 0.18, 0.84).
* Environmental information: collinearity is moderate and stable (max VIF 3.6-4.2; |cor| between TP spread and TP position 0.31-0.44; condition number <= 4.4). **Matched-TP-contrast support exists only up to k = 5**: the number of common supported spread intervals is 246, 171, 38 and 4 at k = 2, 3, 4, 5 (widest interval 1.25, 1.05, 0.45 and 0.25 log10 units; 98, 18, 19 and 12 profiles) and **zero from k = 6 on**, although sharing probability is still 0.69 at k = 6 and the orders to 12 pass the 0.10 sharing criterion. The displayed orders (macroinvertebrates 2 and 5; zooplankton 2 and 4) all have matched support, but k = 5 is close to the minimum (12 of the required 10 profiles over a 0.25-wide interval). Orders 6-12 are statistically usable for the composition-only analyses but cannot support the matched TP contrast of Figure 6.

## Limitations
* The 30 splits are not independent; intervals quantify split-to-split variability only.
* Random validation does not remove spatial dependence; the spatial-block results remain the robustness analysis.
* Figure 5 panels C and D and the Figure 6 figures are all-site descriptive fits; fold/split-level uncertainty of the conditional curves is not computed.
* Model-equivalence checks cover EPA shared-pool data, one sampled set per comparison, and the reference models' supported structures only.
* Full-pool results are in the tables with the same file names (`*_full.csv`) and are not interpreted here.
