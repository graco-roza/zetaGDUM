# Feasibility of spatiotemporal MS-GDUM

The balanced-panel experiment supports three distinct summaries of commonness. Spatial occupancy counts sampled sites. Temporal persistence records sharing across repeat surveys at fixed sites. Conditional abundance measures counts or positive amounts in occupied sites. The measures respond differently to controlled processes, and incidence-based zeta and the existing spatial GDUM definitions can be carried into the extension.

The fitted transition model recovered planted effects at site level with complete detection and a known species pool. It improved predictive log loss at spatial orders one and two when the data contained a within-predictor interaction. Imperfect detection generated both false temporal events and spurious environmental slopes. Higher-order observations were dependent and rapidly lost information about a common position effect. These results support a staged extension using a fixed panel and explicitly specified observation assumptions.

## Existing MS-GDUM components

The implementation was based on development commit `a49359d`. In `ugdm_design()`, the position term is the sum of standardized predictor values over selected sites. Spread is the mean pairwise absolute difference of three I-spline basis values per predictor. Geographic spread is optional. In `ugdm_response()`, native total incidence dissimilarity is one minus shared richness divided by mean site richness, and native Simpson-type dissimilarity uses minimum site richness in the denominator. The difference is native nestedness-resultant dissimilarity. These are set-level compositional uniqueness responses. Numeric position, environmental spread, and compositional uniqueness therefore play different roles.

The spatial-only path calls `ugdm_design()`, `ugdm_response()` and `ugdm_fit()` directly, using a common reference over the observed interval for snapshot comparisons. The operational meaning of native abundance responses is retained, including relative abundance based on summed shared minima and the existing raw-count responses. Experimental positive-abundance and relative-dominance summaries have separate estimands. Native dominance zeta selects dominant species within assemblages by a cumulative abundance threshold.

## Joint zeta and retention

Let $Y_{its}$ denote the recorded presence of species $s$ at site $i$ and survey $t$. For site set $I$ of size $k$ and time set $J$ of size $m$, define

$$
Z(I,J)=\sum_s\prod_{i\in I}\prod_{t\in J}Y_{its}.
$$

A rectangle contains the same sites at every selected survey. The balanced mean $\zeta_{k,m}$ is the equally weighted mean over the $\binom{n}{k}\binom{T}{m}$ rectangles. The cases $(1,1)$, $(2,1)$, $(k,1)$, $(1,2)$, $(1,m)$ and $(k,m)$ represent local richness, pairwise spatial sharing, spatial zeta, local endpoint persistence, sharing over multiple local surveys, and joint spatial and temporal sharing, respectively. At $m=1$ a slice equals ordinary spatial zeta at that time. The decline surface averages that slice over all surveys. This extends the shared-richness formulation of zeta diversity. [Hui and McGeoch (2014)](https://doi.org/10.1086/678125)

For each $J$, define $o_s(J)=\sum_i\prod_{t\in J}Y_{its}$. The number of $k$-site sets sharing species $s$ across $J$ is $\binom{o_s(J)}{k}$. Therefore

$$
\zeta_{k,m} = \frac{1}{\binom{T}{m}}\sum_{J:|J|=m}\sum_s
\frac{\binom{o_s(J)}{k}}{\binom{n}{k}}.
$$

The spatial and temporal retention rates at $(k,m)$ are $\zeta_{k,m}/\zeta_{k-1,m}$ and $\zeta_{k,m}/\zeta_{k,m-1}$ when the respective predecessor exists and is positive. Both are bounded by one since deleting a uniformly chosen site or time from a uniformly chosen rectangle of higher order gives a uniform rectangle of the preceding order, whose shared species set contains the original one. The ratios weight predecessor rectangles by their shared richness. With uneven survey spacing, the complete temporal surface averages over survey subsets with mixed lags. Consecutive-only or lag-matched subsets are different summaries and can be enumerated explicitly with `time_sets`.

The code evaluates the exact mean in work proportional to $\sum_{m \le m_{\max}} \binom{T}{m}(m n S + k_{\max} S)$, for $S$ species. Explicit rectangles cost more and retain identities. The time-combination and rectangle budgets prevent accidental combinatorial explosion. Timings for exact surfaces over five surveys and 48 species were roughly hundredths of a second at 40–320 sites. At 320 sites the single $(7,5)$ cell represented about $6.38\times10^{13}$ rectangles. Exact temporal enumeration still grows combinatorially. The prototype also accepts explicit site sets for transition models, but supplies no proposal-weighted transition estimator.

## Directional events and sampled occupancy

For each ordered pair $t<u$ and fixed $I$, let $A_t$ and $A_u$ be the species shared throughout $I$ at the respective surveys. Persistence $P=|A_t\cap A_u|$, gain $G=|A_u\setminus A_t|$ and loss $L=|A_t\setminus A_u|$ satisfy $Z_t=P+L$, $Z_u=P+G$, and $Z_u-Z_t=G-L$ exactly. The ledger preserves the species and sites in every set. At order one this is the raw incidence accounting behind a gain/loss decomposition of temporal dissimilarity. Higher orders record changes in shared occupancy. [Legendre (2019)](https://doi.org/10.1002/ece3.4984)

With three or more selected surveys, all-survey persistence means membership at every selected time. Endpoint persistence needs membership at both endpoints. Interrupted endpoint persistence includes an observed interior absence. Recolonisation is a sampled transition from sharing to absence and back, including episodes that occur wholly within the endpoints. An intervening-only species was shared on at least one internal survey and missing at both endpoints. Any gaps between selected survey times are unobserved for this history. Even adjacent survey records do not establish ecological continuity between visits.

For species $s$, let $U$, $V$ be the spatial occupancy sets before and after, with sizes $o_0$, $o_1$ and overlap $h$. $V\setminus U$, $U\setminus V$ and $U\cap V$ give newly occupied, lost and persistent sites. Net expansion and contraction follow the sign of $o_1-o_0$. Both gains and losses indicate a change in the identity of occupied sites alongside any net expansion or contraction. Their minimum counts how many sites can be balanced as replacements, without assuming any matching of lost to gained sites or a mechanism of movement. Regional appearance and disappearance are zero-to-positive and positive-to-zero occupancy in the retained panel.

Write $q_k(o)=\binom{o}{k}/\binom{n}{k}$. The species contributes:

- $q_k(o_0)$ and $q_k(o_1)$ to the two means.
- $q_k(h)$ to the mean persistent shared richness.
- $q_k(o_1)-q_k(h)$ and $q_k(o_0)-q_k(h)$ to mean gains and losses.
- $q_k(o_1)-q_k(o_0)$ to net change.

Summing over species recovers the corresponding mean across spatial combinations. Pure redistribution changes $h$ and thus the gross gains and losses even though it leaves all complete-sampling spatial zeta means unchanged. It can change particular combinations and spatial beta. Sampled occupancy is a panel count. Inference about geographic range limits, area or migration would require a sampling design covering the relevant geography and suitable detection or range models.

## Positive abundance and dominance

At each survey $N_s=o_s\mu_s^+$, where $N_s$ is total abundance and $\mu_s^+$ is mean positive abundance. When $o_s=0$, the total is zero and the conditional mean is undefined. Endpoint changes in the positive mean are undefined for appearances and disappearances. Regional numerical dominance is $N_s/\sum_v N_v$, undefined when the whole panel has zero total abundance. A species can lose dominance as other species increase.

A second additive accounting tracks abundance gained at newly occupied sites, abundance lost at vacated sites, and the numerical change at persistently occupied sites. Their signed sum is $\Delta N_s$. Comparing overall positive means can mix changes in local abundance with changes in which sites contribute. At order one, the model can fit the final positive abundance with a Gamma working distribution and log link, using either all final presences or persistent site–species observations. The paired variant fits $\log(N_{u}/N_t)$ at individual site–species observations positive at both endpoints using a Gaussian working model with identity link. These are different conditional means. Both are point models or site-block jackknife fits. Their selection on presence makes them unsuitable for interpreting effects among unobserved or newly absent individuals. A hurdle count model, a detection component, or a positive-abundance offset for known sampling effort would require a separate observation model.

## Spatial beta decomposition

For any pair let $a$ be shared richness and $b,c$ the exclusive richnesses. Pairwise Sørensen dissimilarity is $(b+c)/(2a+b+c)$; Simpson replacement is $\min(b,c)/(a+\min(b,c))$; nestedness-resultant dissimilarity is their difference. The package also reports Jaccard replacement plus richness difference, which answers a different question. Denominators of zero yield undefined components. For example, one empty and one nonempty site yields Sørensen 1 and undefined Simpson and nestedness in the native convention. [Baselga (2010)](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1466-8238.2009.00490.x) [Baselga and Leprieur (2015)](https://doi.org/10.1111/2041-210X.12388)

For a fixed set of sites the established multisite Sørensen partition uses redundancy $a_*=\sum_i |S_i|-|\bigcup_i S_i|$, $b_*=\sum_{i<j}\min(b_{ij},c_{ij})$ and $c_*=\sum_{i<j}\max(b_{ij},c_{ij})$. Its analogue of total dissimilarity is $(b_*+c_*)/(2a_*+b_*+c_*)$, with replacement $b_* /(a_*+b_*)$. The residual equals $a_*(c_*-b_*)/[(a_*+b_*)(2a_*+b_*+c_*)]$ wherever the denominators are positive. Thus the components are nonnegative, bounded by one and additive. Site permutation leaves all three sums invariant. Two sites give the pairwise formulas. A strictly nested chain has $b_*=0$; equal site richness gives $b_*=c_*$. These properties were checked on synthetic cases and compared to `betapart::beta.multi()`. Full-set shared richness alone cannot recover the partition. For instance, three equal-richness sites with species sets $\{a,b\},\{b,c\},\{a,c\}$ have no jointly shared species, native order-three Simpson-type dissimilarity 1 and Baselga multisite Simpson dissimilarity 0.5. This example rules out treating the two multisite estimands as interchangeable. [Baselga (2010)](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1466-8238.2009.00490.x)

Pairwise change accounting evaluates each beta component on four matrices: the initial state $A$, after observed gains alone $A\cup B$, after observed losses alone $A\cap B$, and at the final state $B$. Averaging the contribution of each event type across both possible orders gives two additive contributions that sum to the observed change on pairs whose four component values are defined. The masks are retrospective and can include gain and loss by the same species through redistribution. Changing the order of event masks changes their marginal contributions, so a single fixed ordering would assign their interactions to whichever mask is applied second. The tables report endpoint and full counterfactual support separately. Neither beta trends nor event accounting can assign causal ecological drivers by themselves. Spatiotemporal change may also affect average multivariate dispersion and centroid shifts. [Legendre and Gauthier (2014)](https://doi.org/10.1098/rspb.2013.2728)

## Conditional transition model

For a fixed $k$ and a site set $I$, define $H_{It,s}=\prod_{i\in I}Y_{its}$. Given a declared pool of $S$ species and initial shared richness $Z_t$, the $S-Z_t$ initially unshared species can become shared and the $Z_t$ initially shared species can cease to be shared. Conditional on the initial state, the working likelihood is

$$
G \sim \mathrm{Binomial}(S-Z_t,\gamma),\qquad
L \sim \mathrm{Binomial}(Z_t,\ell),\qquad
\mathrm{logit}(\gamma)=X_\gamma\beta_\gamma,\quad
\mathrm{logit}(\ell)=X_\ell\beta_\ell.
$$

This factors the joint conditional likelihood into two disjoint risk sets under conditional species exchangeability and independence. Persistence and endpoint shared richness follow algebraically. Each event count stays inside its denominator and the fitted expected counts satisfy the event identities. At $k>1$, an initially unshared species can already occupy part of the set. The baseline pattern and potential for establishment affect the risk and are averaged within the fitted pool. One can fit predefined pools, as in the trait simulations. An independently established pool or externally specified availability rule is preferable to choosing species according to future detection. Adding never-available species changes the gain denominator. Boundary fits and zero opportunities are returned explicitly. Mixed outcomes use a small ridge on the coefficients to stabilize separation. Aliased designs return an explicit status and do not generate fitted probabilities. A boundary probability describes the fitted data and is a strong extrapolation when applied outside the observed design.

For each environmental predictor the available columns are initial and final summed positions, their signed change, and the mean absolute site-level change. At orders two and above the corresponding initial and final I-spline spread columns and their signed change are available, calculated on a common basis. Lag uses the declared survey time units. Geographic configuration can enter through mean pairwise geographic distance. The prototype makes the within-predictor interaction explicit as initial position times its change, with main effects specified by the user. All three columns for initial position, final position and signed change are linearly dependent. The analogous identity holds for spread. Lag is aliased with the intercept when every transition has the same duration. Columns such as lag, time and broad climate change may also be statistically confounded in finite panels. Time comparison by itself cannot establish a space–time interaction. The interaction assessed here is a changing environmental response conditional on initial environmental position. Its interpretation requires variation in both position and change on overlapping support. [Legendre, De Cáceres and Borcard (2010)](https://doi.org/10.1890/09-0199.1)

Across sets and transitions, the product of conditional factors is a composite working likelihood. It does not generate a joint panel at multiple orders. In particular independently predicted pair occupancies can violate cross-order marginal constraints. The model does not fit a multinomial over all site configurations. For uncertainty, deleting whole site histories or prespecified spatial blocks removes every overlapping set containing a deleted site. It conditions on survey times, the pool and the predictor basis. Block inference requires approximately independent blocks and enough diverse eligible sets after deletion. It does not address shared temporal shocks across sites, strong dependence between blocks, selection of the species pool, or random effects shared across species or regions. Forecast validation used held-out site histories with train-only environmental scaling. Forecasts condition on observed initial richness for the held-out sets.

## Two-dimensional information diagnostic

Native MS-GDUM order diagnostics use the design rank and curvature of a fitted working response, with site-level perturbations for effects. The experimental diagnostic evaluates an all-survey shared indicator on a common, deliberately simple contrast. For each rectangle, $x$ is the average standardized value of one predictor across its site–time observations. The working model treats the shared count as binomial with $S$ trials and uses an intercept and $x$. With fitted probabilities $p_r$ and weights $w_r=p_r(1-p_r)$, information about the position coefficient after profiling the intercept is $\operatorname{mean}[w_r(x_r-\bar{x}_w)^2]$ per species opportunity. Its ratio to the $(1,1)$ value anchors the surface to a common spatial and temporal scale. The diagnostic also reports design rank, eigenvalue and condition number of the working curvature, and a delete-site jackknife slope standard error where the rectangle enumeration is exhaustive. These are diagnostics for the selected contrast and response. Inherently different estimands such as lag, spread and gains need their own support checks.

Each order also reports the number of species with a positive contribution, effective species $1/\sum_s v_s^2$ for contribution shares $v_s$, and the zero-sharing fraction. The zero fraction is exact within the enumeration budget. Above it, independent uniform rectangles sampled with replacement estimate the fraction, with its Monte Carlo standard error. That error describes computational sampling only. The exact means and species contributions are used at every order. In the example panel $(1,1)$ shared richness averaged 25.68 and had 42.29 effective species. At $(7,5)$ it averaged 1.35, with 6.48 effective species and an estimated 19.4% zero sharing. Position information at $(7,5)$ was 0.96% of the $(1,1)$ value. High retention at some high orders describes the taxa that remain shared. The curvature and species-support surfaces show how little of the original signal those orders retain. Select orders based on the estimand, event support and uncertainty together.

## Simulation results

Every scenario used 32 replicates. The core descriptor scenarios used 30 sites, five surveys and 48 species with unequal baseline occupancies. The positive-abundance experiments include constant positives, shifted Poisson counts and Gamma draws. Trait scenarios cross baseline spatial commonness with a separate persistence trait that reduces loss probability. Trait-targeted recovery uses the true simulated classes. For an empirical test, define classes from independent information or fit a prespecified hierarchical trait model. The scenarios can be combined, as in the expansion, redistribution and abundance experiment. Subsampling and detection experiments retained latent values for paired comparisons.

| Process | Result across balanced panels |
| --- | --- |
| Spatial filtering | Order-two mean turnover spread height was 2.72 under filtering and 0.13 under the null. Mean turnover deviance explained was 0.655 and 0.020, respectively. The monotone nestedness spread fit stayed at its boundary in the filtering scenario. |
| Gains and losses in isolation | Gains-only averaged 10.80 gains and 0 losses per site at the endpoints. Losses-only averaged 5.99 losses and 0 gains. At order six these values fell to 5.82 and 2.36. |
| Expansion and contraction | A mean of 47.06 species expanded in the expansion experiment and 35.69 contracted in the contraction experiment. Mean abundance at persistent sites stayed fixed in each case. |
| Abundance alone | A mean of 47.97 species had changed positive abundance at persistent sites. Occupancy, zeta and incidence beta stayed fixed. |
| Redistribution alone | No species changed net occupancy. At order two, an average of 6.57 gains and 6.57 losses per site pair cancelled exactly at every order in the complete mean. |
| Combined processes | A mean of 46.19 species both expanded in occupancy and changed the identity of occupied sites. Mean positive abundance changed at persistent sites for 47.72 species. |
| Homogenisation and differentiation | Mean pairwise Sørensen changed by −0.395 and +0.318, respectively. The corresponding changes in the full-set multisite index were −0.836 and +0.140. These indices use different normalization. |

Parameter recovery used 44 sites and 64 species with irregular survey intervals. Bias on the fitted link scale was +0.013 for the gain response to change, +0.006 for the loss response, −0.007 for the planted interaction and +0.001 for the positive-abundance response. Bias for targeted gain slopes ranged from −0.039 to +0.024. Targeted effects were detected in every replicate and nominal 95% site-jackknife intervals covered 87.5–100% of these selected true coefficients. Under the clean null, gain and loss change effects had two false rejections each out of 32. These are finite simulation frequencies from a single family of panels. Raw estimates, uncertainty, truth and statuses are in [parameter_recovery.csv](results/parameter_recovery.csv).

In three-fold held-site validation the interaction reduced mean gain log loss from 0.424 to 0.360 at order one and from 0.310 to 0.300 at order two. The log-loss baseline differs between orders because the opportunity response changes. In the separate order-two null simulation, deleting entire sites gave gain and loss standard errors 2.71 and 2.74 times the independent-trial values on average. Treating pairs as independent falsely rejected 10/32 gain and 15/32 loss slopes at the nominal 5% level, compared with 2/32 and 3/32 under site deletion. This simulation supports site-based uncertainty for panels with approximately independent sites. Separate-region or separate-survey holdouts would be needed to evaluate transfer to new regions or future time periods. [Held-out scores](results/site_holdout.csv) · [Dependence diagnostics](results/overlap_dependence.csv) · [Spatial recovery](results/spatial_recovery.csv)

Variable effort was linked to the environmental gradient through effort $e$ and a species-visit detection probability $1-(1-p)^e$. A missed detection sets the whole species count at that site visit to zero. Missing visits were generated separately at the site–time level. In the static-occupancy observation experiment, the complete-site subset averaged 27.31 of 48 sites and the observed mean was 5.10 apparent gains and 4.87 apparent losses per site, both zero in matched latent occupancy. In the coefficient experiment both apparent slopes were significant in 32/32 runs despite zero true occupancy effects. Thinning, loss of sites and environment-dependent effort undermine biological interpretation even after selecting complete sites. Repeated within-survey detection histories or external detection calibration are needed to estimate ecological occupancy separately from detectability. [MacKenzie et al. (2003)](https://doi.org/10.1890/02-3090)

## Unresolved inference and scope

| Design or model limitation | Consequence |
| --- | --- |
| Single visit per site and survey, with unknown detection | Ecological gains and losses, abundance trends and changing detection are confounded. |
| Two endpoint surveys or gaps between surveys | Temporary disappearance, recolonisation and true duration of uninterrupted occupancy can remain unobserved. |
| Fixed, short lag or synchronized environmental change | Lag, final conditions and change may share the same design columns or lack independent support. |
| Few sites, few blocks or strong regional shocks | Overlap correction alone provides weak or invalid population-level uncertainty. |
| Gap-free site subsets from an informative missingness process | The complete-site estimand applies to the retained panel. Extrapolation to dropped sites requires sampling or observation assumptions. |
| Fixed species pool under mixed availability | The gain estimate is conditional on how availability is represented. Pool size and possible arrivals require external identification. |
| Very high spatial or temporal order | Small positive counts, narrow environmental support and a few common species can dominate the result. |
| Endpoint zeta or beta summaries alone | Redistribution can cancel in a mean and distinct trajectories can produce the same index. Species and site histories retain the necessary information. |
| Pooled site-set transition model | Mixture over partially occupied states and species heterogeneity can change coefficients with order. Local transitions and species-level risk models are candidates for jointly coherent cross-order forecasts. |

The tested alias cases demonstrate why initial, final and change terms cannot all enter the same linear model. The independent-pair analysis failed its null calibration. Applying the native higher-order full-intersection response as if it were Baselga's multisite partition fails even for three sites with equal richness. The simulated detection experiment shows why adding an environmental covariate to a model for recorded transitions is insufficient to identify occupancy dynamics. Estimating transitions on incomplete panels, availability, joint dynamic abundance and latent occupancy, temporal shock uncertainty, and rare-event inference at large orders remain research tasks.

Keep the extension inside the MS-GDUM repository as an isolated experimental module for now. Its spatial special case already uses native design, response and fit functions, and the balanced-panel results justify retaining that common implementation. Revisit the package boundary if a latent observation model, substantially different data structures, or a separate dependency stack becomes central. The next statistical comparison is a site-and-species transition model with interpretable random effects and an explicit detection component, aggregated to predicted spatial and temporal zeta. That would provide a test of cross-order coherence against the current composite fit.
