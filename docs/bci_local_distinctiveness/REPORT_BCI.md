# Report: what local multisite metrics add to LCBD at BCI

Data and methods are documented in `README.md`; manuscript-ready paragraphs and captions are in `MANUSCRIPT_TEXT_BCI.md`. Main figure: `figures/Figure4_main.{pdf,svg,png}` (layout A LCBD map, B characteristic retention order map, C retention profiles, D LCBD versus T). Supporting figures `FigureS1-S6`.

## Reproduction of the published analysis
Tree-record counts (status A, dbh ≥ 10 mm) reproduce Legendre & Condit (2019): 244,015 individuals in 1990, +1,354 swamp and −35,697 non-swamp individuals 1985-2015. Hellinger LCBD equals `adespatial::beta.div` exactly. With 99,999 permutations and Holm correction, 1985 has 14 significant quadrats, 12 of them swamp; 2015 has 2, none in the swamp (126 and 26 swamp quadrats significant before correction), the pattern described in the paper. The occurrence LCBD (same transformation on presence-absence) flags 30 of 30 swamp quadrats at p ≤ 0.05 in 1985 and 26 in 2015; after Holm correction 24 quadrats remain in 1985 (18 swamp) and 12 in 2015 (7 swamp), so the occurrence signal of the swamp persists in 2015 whereas the abundance signal does not. Stems instead of tree records change nothing material (LCBD rho 0.97-0.98; occurrence results identical), so stem counts are not needed. Limits: 322 versus 328 species in the 1985/2015 union; the 7-category habitat file cannot be matched to the paper's six groups (500 quadrats in group 1 is not reproducible), only the swamp (30 quadrats).

## Characteristic (T) versus conditional (T+) retention
* **T** = sum over orders of the local conditional zeta profile (expected number of orders over which a site's composition survives in sets that contain it). It is dominated by what is lost at order two, so for relative abundance it is a near-monotone function of pairwise distinctiveness (rho with LCBD = −0.86; 78% of variance explained by LCBD). What it adds: with **occurrence** data it is unrelated to LCBD (rho = −0.03).
* **T+** = 1 + (T − 1)/ζ2|i conditions on sharing at order two. It asks how persistently the part that is shared survives, independently of how little is shared. In the swamp T and T+ are decoupled (rho −0.08; −0.76 elsewhere). Distinctive quadrats retain their shared residue longer (T+ versus LCBD rho 0.84): the shared part consists of widespread dominants.
Neither is a measure of conservation value or irreplaceability.

## Findings by question (matched representations)
Every comparison pairs like with like: abundance LCBD (Hellinger, abundance) with relative-abundance LCZD/T; occurrence LCBD (Hellinger on presence-absence, identical to Sørensen-based ranking, rho = 1.0) with incidence LCZD/T. The two LCBDs agree only moderately (rho 0.57 in 1985, 0.60 in 2015) and both single out the swamp (97% and 100% of swamp quadrats in the top decile).
1. **Persistence across orders.** Abundance: LCZD stays aligned with LCBD (rho 0.93 at k=2, 0.84 at k=5, 0.62 at k=80, 0.53 at k=320); swamp in the top decile 93% (k=2) and 83% (k=80). Occurrence, using the richness-normalised incidence LCZD (sampled sets, exact at k=2, seed-to-seed rho ≥ 0.97): rho with occurrence LCBD 1.00 (k=2), 0.85 (k=5), 0.18 (k=20), −0.22 (k=80), so occurrence distinctiveness is much less persistent across orders than abundance distinctiveness. The swamp nevertheless remains distinctive: mean percentile 0.98 (LCBD), 0.96 (k=5), 0.86 (k=20), 0.75 (k=80); top decile 97% (k=2) and 60% (k=80). The earlier unnormalised (richness-unit) incidence LCZD gave a swamp percentile of 0.53 at k=80 and 0.12 at k=640, which was largely a richness-scale effect. LCZD relative versus normalised incidence: rho 0.43 (k=2), 0.31 (k=80), top-decile Jaccard 0.30-0.37.
2. **Similar LCBD, different retention.** Abundance: T is a near-function of LCBD (rho −0.86, R² 0.78; quadrats within 2% of LCBD differ by a median 0.08 orders, q95 0.50). Occurrence: LCBD explains 20% of T (rho +0.39); pairs within 2% differ by a median 1.6 (q95 5.1) orders. Pairs matched on both LCBDs within 3% (10,671 pairs): median |ΔT| 0.06 (abundance) versus 1.5 (occurrence), q95 0.33 versus 4.6. Example: x005_y024 (slope, 73 species) and x014_y012 (mixed, 28 species): LCBD abundance 0.98/0.99 and occurrence 1.00/1.01 (×10⁻³), abundance T 9.0/9.0, occurrence T 9.1/21.6. A swamp and a slope quadrat (x020_y012, x023_y005; abundance LCBD 1.39/1.41, occurrence 1.07/1.07) have abundance T 7.0/8.2 and occurrence T 20.2/13.2. The differences are in species occupancy (which species are shared and how widely), not in relative abundance.
3. **Swamp.** Distinctive in both representations (Cliff's δ 0.97 abundance, 0.98 occurrence), unrepresentative in abundance (T δ −0.91) but more retentive in occurrence (T δ +0.57; T+ δ +0.88), because swamp quadrats are species-poor (35 versus 54 species, 95 versus 193 trees) subsets with unusual proportions. The swamp is not a distinct species pool.
4. **Abundance versus occurrence.** LCBD abundance versus occurrence rho 0.57; T 0.10; T+ 0.22; LCZD relative versus richness-normalised incidence rho 0.43 (k=2) and 0.31 (k=80), top-decile overlap (Jaccard) 0.30-0.37. The representations give different maps of retention (Fig. 4B).
5. **Species.** Occupancy-restricted: *Bactris major* (57 quadrats, 63% of swamp quadrats) contributes 0.095 of the swamp mean expected dissimilarity (0.71) at k=2. Dominance: *Faramea occidentalis* (all quadrats, 12% of swamp individuals versus 10% elsewhere) contributes 0.045 (k=2) and 0.105 (k=40), 99% and 86% through abundance heterogeneity. The heterogeneity share of local dissimilarity is lowest in the swamp (0.33 versus 0.38-0.57 at k=2) and falls with order (0.15-0.28 at k=40): differentiation at high orders is increasingly occupancy. The swamp indicator *Elaeis oleifera* (1.1% of quadrats, 37% of swamp quadrats) ranks 23rd (0.009, occupancy); the top ten species account for 39% of the swamp total, so the signal is the joint distribution of restricted occupancy and unusual dominance.
6. **Census years.** Swamp LCBD fell 1.78 → 1.33 (abundance) and 1.24 → 1.05 × 10⁻³ (occurrence); others unchanged. Swamp quadrats in the top 5%: 26 → 20 (abundance), 26 → 19 (occurrence). Swamp abundance T rose by 2.0 (0.7-0.8 elsewhere) but stayed near the bottom (percentile 0.06 → 0.08); occurrence T rose only 1.6 (3.5-4.7 elsewhere), cancelling its above-average retention (percentile 0.78 → 0.48). Quadrat-level stability: LCBD rho 0.66 (abundance) / 0.53 (occurrence); T 0.63 / 0.39 (swamp: 0.75 / 0.36 and 0.81 / 0.12). Swamp quadrats gained 11 species and 35 trees (median) whereas other habitats lost trees.

## Informative exceptions (see also tables/exceptions_top5pct_LCBD*.csv)
* 37 of the 63 top-5% LCBD quadrats are not swamp (mostly slope and low plateau). Their occurrence-based T covers almost the whole plot range (percentile 0.001-0.99). Slope quadrats x047_y017 and x043_y025 combine top-5% LCBD with the lowest occurrence-based T (percentile < 0.01): distinct and unrepresentative by every measure; quadrats x015_y014 and x017_y015 (mixed habitat) combine top-5% LCBD with T in the top 10%: distinct yet strongly retained.
* Slope quadrat x004_y023 (LCBD top 1%) has the highest conditional retention T+ (abundance) of the plot, higher than any swamp quadrat.
* Distinctiveness does not imply low retention: among the 63 quadrats in the top 5% of occurrence LCBD, 84% have occurrence T above the plot median (median percentile 0.82) and only one (swamp x014_y014) is in the lowest decile; T+ is positively related to both LCBDs (rho 0.74 occurrence, 0.84 abundance).

## Negative findings and caveats
* For relative abundance, T carries little information beyond LCBD (rho −0.86); the added value is T+, occurrence, and the order-resolved LCZD.
* The richness-normalised incidence LCZD is a Monte Carlo estimate (exact only at order 2; reliability checked by two independent replicates, seed-to-seed rho 0.97-1.00, orders 2-80 only). The analytical incidence LCZD in richness units is kept as a sensitivity column. Species contributions for incidence remain in richness units because no analytical per-species normalised form is available.
* Swamp quadrats hold half as many individuals as other quadrats, so part of their distinctiveness may reflect sampling variance (small samples inflate Hellinger distances); no rarefaction was applied.
* Retention orders depend on plot extent, quadrat size and the number of quadrats (n = 1250); absolute orders are not transferable, rank comparisons are.
* The habitat map has 7 categories rather than the paper's 6 groups; only the swamp and total were verified.
* Incidence local species contributions are in richness units (unnormalised), not directly comparable to relative-abundance contributions.
* Census comparison confounds real change with the overall 14.9% stem decline (Legendre & Condit); metrics are relative within a census, so T values across censuses are not on a common absolute scale.
* Species contributions use a per-species call of the compiled routine behind `conditional_zeta()` (internal function, no package change); it sums exactly to the exported LCZD (error 1e-15) for relative abundance and incidence.
