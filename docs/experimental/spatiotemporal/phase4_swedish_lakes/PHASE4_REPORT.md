# Phase IV. Compositional change in Swedish monitoring lakes

The three lake assemblages changed differently depending on how composition was measured. At pairwise spatial order, the 1995–1999 to 2018–2022 comparison showed incidence differentiation in phytoplankton and littoral macroinvertebrates, and essentially zero net incidence change in profundal macroinvertebrates. Relative composition homogenised in all three on the primary panels, with a small and design-sensitive phytoplankton estimate. Numerical changes in **relative shares among persistent occurrences** made the largest gross contribution in both directions for relative-abundance dissimilarity. Local temporal reorganisation remained substantial even where net spatial change was small.

### Processes and the crossed matrix

| Pairwise relative-composition contribution | Phytoplankton | Littoral | Profundal |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Expansion | −0.0067 | +0.0090 | +0.0067 |
| Contraction | +0.0077 | −0.0083 | −0.0316 |
| Balanced redistribution | +0.0032 | −0.0035 | −0.0086 |
| Persistent relative shares | −0.0115 | −0.0504 | −0.0405 |

Expansion and contraction opposed each other in relative composition, with reversed directions between phytoplankton and both benthic assemblages. For incidence, littoral expansion and contraction both contributed positively. Profundal incidence expansion and contraction were approximately +0.037 and −0.037, explaining the near-zero net. Balanced redistribution was smaller on average but meaningful, particularly in profundal relative composition. Gross contributions were calculated before averaging signed values. Among the homogenising attributions, persistent relative shares accounted for **40%, 72% and 49%** of the gross magnitude, respectively. They also had the largest shares of the differentiating magnitude, **30%, 54% and 46%**. These are separate within-sign percentages.

Expansion affected both columns of the crossed partition. At \((2,1)\) its occupancy-related contributions were −0.0281, −0.0038 and −0.0185, while its numerical-heterogeneity contributions were +0.0214, +0.0128 and +0.0252. Persistent-share changes also reached both columns. In phytoplankton their contribution was −0.0196 to occupancy-related dissimilarity and +0.0081 to numerical heterogeneity. For dominant composition, the persistent-share block was negative in all groups. It opposed the positive sum of the three occupancy blocks in phytoplankton and reinforced their negative sum in the two invertebrate assemblages. [Crossed estimates with uncertainty and shares](results/crossed_summary.csv)

The genus-level sensitivity retained relative-composition homogenisation in all three groups. Phytoplankton incidence changed sign to −0.0195, and profundal genus-level incidence was −0.0304. The primary taxon-resolution incidence patterns therefore depend on taxonomic resolution. Reclassifying after deleting latitude block three gave phytoplankton relative homogenisation of −0.0267. On the 12-lake original-date phytoplankton panel the estimate was +0.0234, while the original 14-lake profundal panel yielded −0.1475. Dates and membership both differ in that sensitivity. For dominance thresholds 0.5/0.8/0.9 on identical sampled pairs, phytoplankton estimates were +0.004/−0.019/−0.049. Both benthic estimates remained negative under all three thresholds. Between the shared minima of lake pairs, temporal relative Bray–Curtis averaged 0.812, 0.576 and 0.585. Local and shared temporal distances answer different questions. Phytoplankton is the clearest case of local reorganisation alongside negligible net spatial change. On the independent fixed annual panels, using 0.02 relative-dissimilarity units as a descriptive near-zero threshold, 17 phytoplankton, 21 littoral and 8 profundal year-to-year contrasts had small net spatial change. Their mean local relative Bray–Curtis distances were 0.561, 0.393 and 0.281. These are descriptive temporal distances and spatial changes, with neither converted into the other.

On the fixed annual panels, pairwise relative dissimilarity between the 1995 and 2022 snapshots changed from 0.826 to 0.841, 0.686 to 0.631, and 0.727 to 0.592. These are two snapshot endpoints on Design B and differ from averages on the larger Design A panels. Annual trajectories show variation between endpoints. The temporal uncertainty table summarises the mean over 27 annual contrasts using four-contrast moving blocks, six spatial deletions, and their combined variance. It does not assign sampling intervals to a single realised annual contrast or to the single five-survey early/late window pair. ## Higher orders and computation

The incidence, relative minimum and dominance zeta surfaces were computed exactly for the early and late pools. Both unrestricted and consecutive survey combinations are reported, with all spatial orders 1–6, temporal orders 1–5, axis-specific retention, species weights and inverse-concentration effective taxa. Figure 2 uses consecutive windows and the main native dominance threshold. Process grids use equally weighted compatible aligned windows, uniform spatial sampling and 32 allocation draws unless exactly enumerable. They share each allocation between metrics and orders. A response's common support requires every coalition and evaluated allocation to be defined. Spatial change and temporal distance support are published separately.

At \((6,5)\) exact late incidence zeta was 1.637, 2.276 and 0.081, with 3.20, 4.54 and 1.54 effective taxa. Profundal shared incidence temporal support was about 4.7%; the initial zero-sharing rate was 97.5% in the independent endpoint sample. At the same order, temporal shared-dominance support was 0.3%, 2.6% and 0%, respectively, even though local dominance remained measurable. We flag weak shared temporal results if common support is below 80%, either effective-taxon count is below two, or initial zero-sharing exceeds 50%. This diagnostic is defined by information and support, without screening on effect direction. Higher orders downweight less ubiquitous taxa and can also lose support across years. These effects do not uniquely identify a class of taxa. [Informative orders](results/informative_orders.csv) · [Exact zeta and retention](results/zeta_surfaces.csv) · [Species weights](results/zeta_species_weights.csv.gz)

After a 160-set pilot, the main analysis increased to three independent 320-draw samples per spatial order and offset, using all sets if fewer existed. On the representative first matched pair at \(k=2,4,6\), the largest change in a mean abundance, occupancy or numerical process effect between 320 and 640 draws was 0.0072. The grid publishes combination MC SE separately and gives each aligned window equal weight on its valid support. Allocation sensitivity used prefixes of 32, 128 and 512 draws. At \((2,1)\) the largest representative allocation MC SE fell from 0.00244 to 0.00053 between 32 and 512 draws. The larger budgets are sensitivity analyses at \((2,1)\) and \((4,3)\), given computational cost. Spatial ecological SEs retain the full matched regional classification and are reported at pairwise spatial order for temporal orders one and five. The block-deletion reclassification sensitivity changes the target regional panel separately. Six latitude blocks and two decades of repeated but overlapping comparisons limit the generality of all interval estimates. ## Validation and interpretation

Squaring and closing the Hellinger values reproduced unit rows to numerical tolerance without reconstructing absolute abundance. Intermediate counterfactuals replace the assigned cells of the original closed relative vectors and then reclose each modified row. Incidence is unaffected by closure and by the numerical intervention block. The compiled Phase III evaluator was checked against the R reference, and the full empirical grid checks Shapley efficiency, the incidence numerical dummy and the eight-entry abundance identity. At pairwise spatial order, native Baselga components agreed exactly on checked defined denominators. The Legendre local incidence sum was checked against gain and loss components; additional temporal dominance distance uses Sørensen on native dominant membership. Tatsumi's published decomposition agreed on net changes to less than \(1.2\times10^{-15}\) for each of the two measures. The comparator was defined for 200/200 phytoplankton, 200/200 littoral and 189/200 profundal pairs per measure. Its individual attributions use different accounting rules. [Mathematical validation](results/mathematical_validation.csv) · [Joint-window validation](results/window_mathematical_validation.csv) · [Local Legendre components](results/local_legendre.csv) · [Tatsumi comparisons](results/pairwise_tatsumi.csv)

Numerical heterogeneity is the minimum-positive-relative-abundance partition from Phase III. On nonempty rectangles it and its occupancy-related partner sum to relative-abundance dissimilarity. The numerical process instead changes shares of persistent occurrences. It may reflect changes elsewhere in the local assemblage because shares are closed. Expanded or contracted occupancy describes the matched sampled lakes, and balanced redistribution records compensating site changes without establishing movement. The phase investigates compositional accounting within observed panels. It supports no claims about absolute biomass, individual population change, detection probabilities or causal drivers. Missing or unverified surveys, taxonomic resolution, support, and the chosen regional panel remain substantive limits.

## Reproduce and browse

Run the numbered scripts in order from the repository root. The installed experimental baseline uses native `zetaGDUM`. Scripts also use `data.table`, `digest`, `xml2`, `stringi`, `sf`, `Rcpp`, `ecopart` and `maps`, plus curl and an R toolchain with Cairo. `ST4_WORKERS` changes parallel workers, while `ST4_BUDGET` and `ST4_DRAWS` change the main computational settings. All nine scripts were executed. The [results directory](results/) includes replicate-level tables and retained lake-set contribution rows for the declared representative orders.
