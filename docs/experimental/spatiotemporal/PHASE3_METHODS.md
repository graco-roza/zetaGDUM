# Phase III methods

Phase III models the ecological-process contributions of a **spatial combination × temporal contrast**. Species and site identities enter the accounting that constructs each response. The Phase I and II implementations and their zeta functions remain archived at their existing paths. Phase III uses a separate loader and fits signed community-level responses.

## Diversity and units

Let $A_{its}$ be absolute abundance for species $s$ at site $i$ and survey $t$, $X=1(A>0)$, and $Q=A/\sum_s A$. Data have matched sites, harmonised taxonomy, a fixed regional species pool, comparable effort and complete detection. In a rectangle $I\times J$, let $n_R=km$, $r_s=\sum X_s$, $p_s=r_s/n_R$ and $H_s=1(r_s=n_R)$. Incidence zeta is $Z=\sum H_s$. Mean richness is $\sum p_s$, and minimum richness is the smallest observation-level richness. Dissimilarities are $1-Z/R_{\mathrm{mean}}$, $1-Z/R_{\min}$, and their difference. At order $(2,1)$ these equal Sørensen, Simpson and nestedness-resultant dissimilarity on defined denominators. At higher orders the full-intersection response differs from Baselga's multisite indices. [Hui and McGeoch (2014)](https://doi.org/10.1086/678125) · [Latombe, Hui and McGeoch (2017)](https://doi.org/10.1111/2041-210X.12756) · [Baselga (2010)](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1466-8238.2009.00490.x)

Abundance zeta is $\sum_s\min Q_s$ over the original normalized site-time observations. Because the means of $Q$ sum to one, its complement is $\sum_s(\overline Q_s-\min Q_s)$, Bray–Curtis on relative abundance at $(2,1)$. For a species occurring at least once, let $\mu_s$ be its mean positive relative abundance and $b_s$ its **minimum positive relative abundance**. Absence throughout contributes zero. Since $\bar Q_s=p_s\mu_s$ and $\min Q_s=H_s b_s$,
$$
d_s=(p_s-H_s)b_s+p_s(\mu_s-b_s).
$$
Both terms are nonnegative since $p_s\ge H_s$ and $\mu_s\ge b_s$. The first describes abundance-weighted incomplete occupancy and the second relative numerical heterogeneity among occupied observations. At $(2,1)$ species exclusive to one site contribute only to the first, and species shared by both sites contribute only to the second. At larger orders incomplete sharing and positive numerical variation can coexist. This partition inherits compositional normalization and a minimum-positive reference convention. Dominance zeta applies the incidence definition to native local dominance at the 0.8 cumulative threshold.

## Whole-panel intervention blocks

Pair survey positions across equal-size, ordered, non-overlapping windows. For every matched survey pair and species, identify gained sites $G$, lost sites $L$, and persistent positive occurrences across the entire matched site panel. The excess gains $[g-l]_+$ form expansion, excess losses $[l-g]_+$ form contraction, and matched counts of gains and losses $\min(g,l)$ form redistribution. This last count represents balanced changes of occupied site identity, with no correspondence inferred between particular sites. The net occupancy budget is $g-l=E-C$ and the gross event budget is $g+l=E+C+2R$. Regional appearance and disappearance are labels on the complete matched panel for each pair. Numerical change covers only positive-to-positive cells.

For a species with unequal positive gain and loss counts, a majority-site subset of size $|g-l|$ is selected for expansion or contraction. All remaining changed occupancy cells belong to redistribution. The product of the binomial coefficients for these independent subset choices gives the number of allocations. Each is equally weighted. Exact averaging enumerates the product below a configurable threshold. Uniform independently sampled allocations use a seeded RNG above it and share the same mask across sets, responses and order calls with the same settings. The module reports the across-allocation SD and, under sampling, its Monte Carlo SE. Coherent aggregate MC error requires first aggregating each draw, since the same mask is used throughout the panel. Exact Shapley applies to each allocation even when its allocation average is sampled.

For every subset of the four blocks, apply its absolute abundance replacements to the initial rectangle panel and recompute normalization and dominance. The empty and full subsets reproduce the initial and final communities. Shapley credits each block the average of its incremental diversity changes over all 24 block orders, computed by weighting its eight subset differences by $|S|!(3-|S|)!/4!$. The implementation evaluates all 16 values, with identical counterfactuals cached when some blocks are empty. This preserves interaction terms and adds exactly to endpoint change within numerical precision. Incidence and incidence-temporal responses have a numerical dummy block. The crossed abundance partition is the 4 × 2 matrix of Shapley values for occupancy-related and numerical dissimilarity. With common support its sum is the total abundance-dissimilarity change, and its row sums are the four total-abundance process effects.

A response and combination require a finite value for every subset in every evaluated allocation. The module retains endpoint values and reports the fraction of evaluated allocations with complete support, with all four contributions missing when support fails. For sampled allocations, future draws can reveal previously unevaluated undefined subsets; the support flag refers to the evaluated masks. Relative abundance is undefined when any included original or counterfactual observation is empty. Incidence ratios are undefined for zero denominators. We never select different combinations for different subsets of a given decomposition.

## Spatial change and temporal reorganisation

The first estimand is signed change in spatial or joint space–time differentiation. The second is temporal Sørensen between the two shared incidence vectors or Bray–Curtis between the two shared relative-abundance minimum vectors. Each is a separate set function relative to the fixed initial community. A third, site-resolved summary averages dissimilarity over matched site × survey positions and preserves the individual site results. Distance between joint shared vectors can miss reorganisation of local composition. Two all-zero shared vectors give an undefined temporal distance. Signed Shapley values for an endpoint temporal distance still sum to that distance. [Legendre (2019)](https://doi.org/10.1002/ece3.4984)

Under complete uniform spatial sampling at $m=1$, each species contributes $q_k(o)=\binom{o}{k}/\binom{n}{k}$ to mean incidence zeta. Net expansion increases this value, contraction decreases it, and a balanced redistribution keeps it fixed. The result holds for the mean even when individual combinations change. At higher temporal order, balanced changes in each survey can change the occupation of the all-survey intersection. More surveys therefore do not isolate temporal persistence from spatial occupancy. Time positions, spacing, durations, and lag are explicit alignment choices.

The pairwise benchmark uses Tatsumi's published `ecopart::ecopart.pair()` for Sørensen and Bray–Curtis. For a matched abundance comparator normalize each observed assemblage before passing it to the published pairwise implementation. Preserve positive and negative components separately. Component magnitudes can differ between the two accounting schemes because their reference and allocations differ. Neither partition identifies causal effects. [Tatsumi, Iritani and Cadotte (2021)](https://doi.org/10.1111/ele.13720) · [Tatsumi, Iritani and Cadotte (2022)](https://doi.org/10.1111/2041-210X.13921)

## Community-level environmental model

Each response row is one spatial combination crossed with one temporal comparison. The model takes four signed process contributions for a single metric or eight crossed abundance contributions. Let $P_v$ be the native sum of standardized values and $S_{vb}$ the native mean pairwise absolute I-spline difference. For windows, predictor values are averaged per site before the position and I-spline calculations. The shared design uses intercept, $P_v$, $\Delta P_v$, $S_{vb}$, $\Delta S_{vb}$ and log mean paired-site survey lag. One training-only basis is reused across responses and spatial and temporal orders. Constant columns are omitted. Spatial spread is structurally absent for one-site combinations. Position uses sums for compatibility; curves change the **mean** per-site environmental contrast in standardized units across orders. [Hernández-Carrasco et al. (2026)](https://doi.org/10.1111/ele.70338)

Fit the signed linear working model with a common weight vector and ridge penalty:
$$
\widehat B=(X^\mathsf{T} W X+\lambda K)^{-1}X^\mathsf{T} W Y,
$$
where $K$ is diagonal with the intercept unpenalized. Components share their support and penalty; coefficient estimates are linear in $Y$. Summing predicted components therefore reproduces the directly fitted total-change prediction from the same weighted ridge system. The primary analysis uses equal weights and $\lambda=0.5$. Coefficients are signed, including I-spline coefficients. The only secondary interaction is $P_v\Delta P_v$, included with both main effects. Rank and singular values describe the design before penalization; the effective degrees of freedom are the trace of the penalized hat matrix. Profiled position and spread information are mean residualized design curvature per observation and column. These curvature quantities alone do not calibrate ecological uncertainty.

A predicted normalized dissimilarity can fall outside $[0,1]$ when its signed predicted change is added to the observed initial value. Individual Shapley values outside $[-1,1]$ also violate the per-response bound for bounded diversity. The implementation flags these predictions and values outside training design ranges. It preserves the unmodified predictions. Sign summaries are in original diversity units: positive magnitude, absolute negative magnitude, their difference, and their sum. Relative importance divides component magnitude by the gross magnitude when positive. Mean absolute effects across combinations are reported separately from absolute mean effects.

## Validation estimands

The primary simulation uses 20 sites and 16 surveys in eight predeclared paired contrasts, five spatial blocks, and 12 fixed-pool species. It uses 200 fitted panels per scenario, 240 independently seeded panels for ecological contrast reference, and 32 bootstrap draws. Spatial and temporal covariates share a fixed design across these panels, and positive anchors retain comparable denominators. A calibration contrast compares mean component contributions in the upper and lower quarters of site-set mean climate change on that fixed design. It is an associational design contrast that also averages accompanying position and spread differences. The independent-panel reference uses the same set/window schedule and observed contribution definition. True zero effects for the clean and shock nulls follow from independent exchangeable process generation relative to the covariates; Monte Carlo estimates for them remain visible. Nominal coverage uses the plug-in truth for non-null effects and reports reference Monte Carlo SE separately.

The critical zero-net generator has two site types, nonempty anchors and a focal species that expands from one type to both. Set $a>0$ to be the focal absolute abundance and $d=a/(2+a)$. Initial sites have vectors $(1,1,a)$ or $(1,1,0)$. The expansion-only state is $(1,1,a)$ at both types. The final vectors are $(h,l,a)$ and $(l,h,a)$ with $(h-l)/(h+l+a)=d$. Abundance dissimilarity is $d$ both initially and finally in any set containing both types and is zero in a homogeneous set. The numerical-only state provides the other pathway for exact four-block Shapley averaging. The generator varies $d$, the final common total and the climate-associated site types across panels and comparisons. Its exact cancellation is an order-$m=1$ analogue; joint multi-survey windows can differ in average and minimum abundances across times.

The broader grid tests spatial orders 2–6 and temporal orders 1–4 on 12 independent panels per scenario, with 26 site sets per cell. It uses the full response family and explicitly records undefined support, timing, retained fit-object size, rank, profiled information and predictive contrast uncertainty when at least four temporal comparisons are available. The holdout study uses 24 additional panels per scenario. Site holdouts use complete histories, with process classifications computed separately within the training and test site panels to avoid leakage of held-out outcomes into classification. This can change the regional boundary of the accounting estimand. Forward holdouts train on the first 12 surveys and test on the final four. Raw outcomes are scored against both the additive and interaction fits and an intercept-only comparator. The timing sensitivity changes window alignment and gap as well as numeric spacing; irregular time labels with unchanged survey indices intentionally leave the community calculation unchanged.

The uncertainty comparison refits the same ridge model after deleting every set containing a member of a spatial block. The moving-block bootstrap samples consecutive full contrasts with replacement, keeping each set and site's original chronology. The crossed bootstrap multiplies temporal replicate weights by multinomial weights for the distinct spatial blocks represented by a set. Independent panels calibrate the resulting Wald intervals and supply the empirical SD alongside mean SE and rejection and coverage Monte Carlo errors. Near-zero effects and unavailable high-order support limit interpretation; exact engine identities and out-of-sample fit do not establish inferential calibration.
