# Sampling requirements of additive MS-GDUM: sample size, zeta order and environmental design

Branch `final-empirical-analysis`; scripts in this directory (`run_all.sh`; run from `final/`, `ZG_LIB6` = principal additive package, branch `principal/additive-msgdum`). No explicit interactions are used anywhere. All numbers below come from `tables/`; figures are in `figures/` (pdf/svg/png). The manuscript and the empirical analyses are untouched.

## Questions and how they are separated
1. **Recovery** - can additive MS-GDUM recover known TP-spread and TP-position effects? *Known-parameter experiment* (`11_known_rep.R`): responses are generated from an additive MS-GDUM with known link-scale coefficients defined on a fixed population basis, binomial noise (300 trials) and a site-level random effect (sd 0.25) on the total and heterogeneity-share logits, so that independent sites, not only combinations, carry information. Truth = the matched-cell prediction machinery applied to the true coefficients on the replicate's own supported reference configurations.
2. **Prediction** - can it predict differentiation at increasing zeta orders? Held-out skill (1 - MSE / variance of the held-out total dissimilarity) on 1,500 random combinations of 400 independent held-out sites (identical in both samplers; the null does not depend on the training sampler), for the coherent additive MS-GDUM and the spread-only MS-GDM fitted on the same combinations with the same penalties.
3. **Support and accuracy of matched contrasts** - does the sampled environmental space support the matched contrasts, and are they accurate? *Site-level metacommunity experiment* (`10_meta_rep.R`): 200 taxa (120 specialists, 80 generalists, Gaussian TP niches; generalist dominance increases with TP), communities generated per site (multinomial, N = 2,000), never per combination. Truth = independent Monte Carlo of the package response functions on freshly simulated communities for the replicate's matched configurations (25 draws per reference configuration), never the fitted model.

**Design (both experiments).** TP (focal) is simulated on the log10 scale of the empirical Figure 6: *uniform* U(0.5, 2.5), *normal* N(1.5, 0.4) truncated to [0.4, 2.8], *clustered* (three clusters at 0.9, 1.55, 2.2, sd 0.1). TN and Chl-a are correlated nuisance predictors (TP-TN and TP-Chl-a correlations about 0.6-0.8); coordinates are random and geographic distance has no true effect. Orders k = 2, 3, 5, 10, 20. Combination sampling: **random** (uniform k-subsets) or **environmentally balanced** (target cells of mean TP x TP spread on a regular grid, arrangement of the profile randomised; each target profile is filled with randomly chosen unused *already-sampled* sites within +-0.03 of the targets and accepted only when the achieved mean and spread are within 0.05; infeasible cells are skipped, never relaxed; the target number of combinations was always reached; the same sites are used, so no extra fieldwork). Random and balanced sampling are **paired**: same sites, communities, held-out data and target number of combinations.
Separation of sites from combinations: **S** (6 site numbers 100-5,000 at 4,000 combinations; 6 replicates), **C** (250-16,000 combinations at 500 sites; 6 replicates) and **F** (6 x 4 factorial at k = 5; 3 replicates). 945 unique units per experiment x 2 samplers (1,890 sampler runs each), 3 environments x 5 orders; nothing was dropped or selected (all unit results are in the tables).

**Support rules - unchanged from Figure 6** (`01_support.R` is the Figure 6 machinery, adapted only to take the sampled combinations as input): TP positions 10, 35, 100 ug/L; reference configurations are observed training combinations with TN, Chl-a, location and the internal arrangement of each profile preserved; >= 10 common reference configurations, >= 3 (here effectively >= 5) grid points, >= 0.20 log10 spread width, >= 2 spread bins, per-predictor cell support (>= 8 sets and >= 12 sites per cell) and joint nearest-neighbour support. **No criterion was relaxed.** A replicate without a valid interval is **unsupported** and is counted as such (it is never imputed, and for "success" it counts as a failure). Contrasts are evaluated in a standard 0.20-wide spread window centred in the widest supported interval: contrast A = spread increase across the window at Low/Intermediate/High TP position; contrast B = Low -> High TP position at the window midpoint (40 evenly selected supported reference configurations).
**Accuracy rule (fixed before inspecting results):** an estimate is *accurate* when |estimate - truth| <= max(1 pp, 25% of |truth|) (pp = percentage points of dissimilarity); sensitivity rules (0.5 pp/15%, 2 pp/50%) are in the tables.

## 3. Does the sampled environmental space support matched contrasts? (`Fig_S1_*`, `Fig_S6_*`, `Fig_S7_*`)
Fraction of replicates with a valid matched interval (series S; pooled over 6 site numbers and 3 environments; metacommunity / known-parameter experiments agree within 0.1):

| order | random | balanced |
|---|---|---|
| 2 | 0.82 | 0.73 |
| 3 | 0.60 | 0.91 |
| 5 | 0.02 | 0.86 |
| 10 | 0.00 | 0.44 |
| 20 | 0.00 | 0.00 |

* **Order 20 is never supported** (0 of 216 replicates in S and none in C up to 16,000 combinations at 500 sites), and random combinations are not supported at k >= 10 (0/216) or, effectively, at k = 5 (2/108). Large-order sets average over the environment, so the spread of a set hardly varies and the same spread/position cells are never reached.
* **Environment matters.** With >= 1,000 sites, balanced sampling gives 100% support up to k = 5 for uniform and normal sites and 89-94% at k = 10; clustered sites reach 100% at k <= 5 but only 39% at k = 10, and clustered random sets are unsupported (0%) at k >= 3 with >= 500 sites (6-17% at 100-250 sites).
* **Sites, not combinations, limit support.** Support rises with the number of sites (balanced, k = 10: 0, 22, 17, 61, 72, 89% at 100-5,000 sites; k = 5: 39%, 78%, 100% at 100, 250, 500 sites), whereas more combinations help only up to about 4,000: 250 combinations give no support in any cell, 1,000 combinations give <= 39% (random, k = 2: 39%; balanced, k = 5: 28%; 500 sites), 4,000 give 100% (balanced, k = 3-5, 500 sites) and 16,000 give no further gain (k = 5: 83%; k = 10: 0% against 17%; with 6 replicates these are noise-level differences). Factorial at k = 5 (`Fig_S6`; pooled over environments, 3 replicates each): with balanced sampling 4,000 combinations support 100% at >= 500 sites and 33% at 100 sites; 16,000 combinations do not help at 100 sites (22%); random sampling supports almost nothing at any combination (max 11% at 5,000 sites).
* **Which criterion binds** (`sim_support_binding_criteria.csv`, replicate 1 of S, diagnostic only): where balanced designs fail at k >= 10 the joint nearest-neighbour support is the sole binding criterion (20/20 cases); for small k and few sites it is the per-cell site count (>= 8 sets and >= 12 sites in a TP cell); random sets at k >= 10 fail several criteria at once (range, cells, neighbours), so removing any one does not help.
* **Interval width.** Supported intervals shrink with order (median width 0.90 log10 at k = 2, 0.60 at k = 3, 0.40 at k = 5, 0.25 at k = 10 under balanced sampling; the supported random k = 5 replicates sit at the 0.20 minimum) and the number of supported reference configurations falls from about 320 (k = 2) to 12 (k = 10) (`sim_*_availability_*.csv`).

## 1. Can MS-GDUM recover known position and spread effects? (known-parameter experiment, `Fig_S2/S3_known_parameter`)
* **Yes, with statistical error that shrinks with the number of independent sites.** For supported replicates the additive model's median absolute error is 0.14 pp (spread contrast at High TP), 0.32 pp (Intermediate), 1.15 pp (Low; bias -1.1 pp) and 0.72 pp (position contrast Low -> High, true median 13.4 pp); the signs are always correct and 98-100% of estimates are accurate (83% for spread at Low TP). At order 2 the position-contrast error falls from 0.98 pp at 100 sites to 0.43-0.46 pp at 2,500-5,000 sites (similar at k = 3 and 5); the link-scale error of the spread contrast is flat after 250 sites (0.012). Combinations have little influence at fixed sites (500 sites, k = 2: 1.07, 0.89 and 0.92 pp at 1,000, 4,000 and 16,000 combinations).
* **The spread-only MS-GDM cannot recover the position effect**: median error 6.4 pp for contrast B (9% accurate; bias -6.4 pp), and it mis-estimates the spread contrast at Low and High TP (4.1 and 2.5 pp). Its error does not fall with n because it has no position terms.
* **Prediction is near the attainable limit** at k <= 5: held-out deviance explained is 0.97-1.00 of the oracle (true-mean) deviance at k = 2-5, 0.91 at k = 10 and 0.76 at k = 20 with random combinations (`D2_share_of_oracle`).

## 3b. Accuracy of matched contrasts in the site-level metacommunity (`Fig_S2/S3/S3b_metacommunity`)
* **Position contrast (B, true change about -24 to -34 pp):** the additive MS-GDUM has the correct sign in every supported replicate but systematically under-estimates its magnitude; median absolute error 5.3 pp (k = 2), 7.3 pp (k = 3), 7.1 pp (k = 5) and 5.7 pp (k = 10; 48 estimates, all balanced). Overall 79% of estimates meet the accuracy rule (99%, 76%, 63%, 58% at k = 2, 3, 5, 10). **The error does not decrease with sample size** (k = 2, pooled over samplers: 4.6, 5.5, 5.1, 5.1, 5.3, 5.6 pp from 100 to 5,000 sites; k = 3: 6.6 -> 7.4 pp; k = 5: 6.0 -> 7.6 pp) in contrast to the known-parameter experiment, so it is a structural error of the additive model class for this generator (strong dependence of the spread effect on TP position through guild dominance; no interaction is allowed in the principal implementation), not sampling error.
* **Spread contrast (A):** well estimated at High TP (balanced error 1.3-3.9 pp; 85% accurate overall), worse at Intermediate (49%) and poor at Low TP (8.1 pp; 32% accurate), growing with order (balanced, Low TP: 1.3, 6.3, 12.8 and 24 pp at k = 2, 3, 5, 10, against true effects of 16-37 pp). The sign is always correct. The occupancy- and heterogeneity-specific contrasts are rarely within tolerance (5-8% for occupancy and 3-17% for heterogeneity for contrast A; 64% and 20% for contrast B), so component-level contrasts should not be interpreted quantitatively in this setting.
* **The spread-only MS-GDM fails on position contrasts** (0% accurate, median error 13.6 pp). **For spread contrasts it is not worse than the additive model at Intermediate and Low TP** (accurate 51% and 57% against 49% and 32%; median error 4.8 and 4.6 pp against 4.8 and 8.1 pp) and clearly worse at High TP (11% against 85%): in this generator the additive position terms do not improve, and at Low TP degrade, the spread-effect estimate. The additive model is needed for position contrasts, but its quantitative accuracy is bounded by the model class.
* **Absolute effects in this generator (12-37 pp per 0.20 log10 spread, 24-34 pp per position change) are much larger than the empirical Figure 6 contrasts (< 1 pp and < 7 pp)**; absolute errors scale with effect size, so relative error (about 20-25% of the position effect; `rel_err_dT`) is the transferable quantity.

## 2. Prediction at increasing zeta order (`Fig_S4_*`)
* **Held-out skill declines with order and is flat in sample size.** Random-combination training (additive MS-GDUM): skill 0.95, 0.92, 0.80, 0.61, 0.47 at k = 2, 3, 5, 10, 20, essentially identical from 100 to 5,000 sites and from 250 to 16,000 combinations (`prediction_combinations`; k = 5: 0.78-0.80). RMSE falls with order only because total dissimilarity saturates near 1 (5.6 pp at k = 2, 0.7 pp at k = 20). The known-parameter experiment shows the same decline (0.90, 0.84, 0.74, 0.46, 0.14).
* **Position terms help prediction mostly at higher orders:** gain over the spread-only MS-GDM +0.009, +0.016, +0.065, +0.21, +0.32 skill at k = 2-20 (positive in 94-100% of replicates; known-parameter: +0.10 to +0.16 at k = 2-20). Where skill is <= 0 (both models worse than the held-out mean) differences between models are not interpretable.
* **Environmentally balanced training harms random-combination prediction at k >= 10** (skill 0.18 at k = 10, -2.7 at k = 20; paired: better in 2% and 0% of replicates), a covariate shift between the balanced design and the random test combinations; at k = 2-5 the two are equivalent (median paired skill difference -0.004, +0.009, +0.002). The package's uniform-target importance weights (`sampling_weights`) were not evaluated here.

## Does environmentally balanced combination sampling help without extra fieldwork?
Using the same sites, communities and number of combinations (540 paired units at 4,000 combinations):
* **Availability:** balanced only 176, both 141, random only 15, neither 208. By order (balanced-only / both / neither / random-only): k = 2: 4/75/15/14; k = 3: 34/64/9/1; k = 5: 91/2/15/0; k = 10: 47/0/61/0; k = 20: 0/0/108/0. Balanced sampling therefore **creates matched contrasts at k = 3-10 that random sampling cannot support**, using only sites already sampled. At k = 2 it is no better and with <= 250 sites it can be worse (balanced 0% against 61% at 100 sites; 67% = random at 250 sites), because the balanced sets concentrate on few sites and leave per-cell site counts below the threshold.
* **Accuracy:** among supported pairs, balanced sets reduce the spread-contrast error (median |error| at Intermediate TP 1.0 vs 2.3 pp at k = 2; 3.9 vs 4.8 pp at k = 3; balanced better in 84% and 82% of pairs) and the position-contrast error at k = 2 (5.3 vs 5.6 pp) but not at k = 3 (7.8 vs 6.6 pp; balanced better in 34%); the k = 5 pairs (3) are too few. Known-parameter experiment: balanced is as accurate as or better than random (position contrast at k = 3: 0.56 vs 0.77 pp). Balancing does not remove the structural error.
* **Prediction:** no benefit and a clear cost at k >= 10 for random-combination targets (above). Environmentally balanced sampling is thus a design for matched *contrasts*, not for prediction under random combination sampling.

## Practical guidance
1. **Check matched support before interpreting position-spread contrasts**; unsupported contrasts must be reported as unsupported, not approximated. In this simulation no contrast is available at k = 20 with up to 5,000 sites, and random-combination designs support contrasts only at k <= 3.
2. **Prefer low orders (k = 2-5) for contrasts.** With balanced combinations (4,000 combinations), support in >= 90% of replicates was reached at about 1,000 sites for k = 2 and 3, 500 sites for k = 5, and 1,000 sites for k = 10 only in uniform/normal environments (89-94%); pooled over environments k = 10 needs 5,000 sites (89%). Clustered environments need >= 1,000 sites for k <= 5 and reach only 67% at k = 10 with 5,000 sites. Random combinations (>= 1,000 sites) are adequate only at k = 2 (and k = 3 in uniform environments).
3. **Combinations: about 4,000 are enough; more adds little**, 250 is never enough and 1,000 is marginal. Extra sites help; extra combinations from the same sites do not.
4. **Use environmentally balanced combinations for contrasts, with random combinations (or reweighting, untested) for prediction;** balanced sampling is not a remedy at k = 2 with few sites.
5. **Do not equate statistical convergence with accuracy.** Contrast errors fall with the number of sites when the additive model is true (known parameters) but plateau at about 20-25% (position) and larger (spread at low TP) when it is not (metacommunity). Component-level (occupancy, heterogeneity) contrasts are not quantitatively reliable here.
6. Spread-only MS-GDM cannot represent position effects (0-9% accurate position contrasts); additive MS-GDUM is required for them, and its position terms improve prediction chiefly at k >= 5, but they did not improve spread contrasts in the metacommunity.

## Limitations
* One metacommunity generator (increasing generalist dominance with TP; N = 2,000 individuals per site; 200 taxa), one known-parameter truth, TP as the focal predictor, a fixed TP position grid (10/35/100 ug/L) and one balancing algorithm (on TP only; TN and Chl-a are not balanced). Absolute effect sizes exceed the empirical ones; percentages depend on that scale. Other generators (weaker or non-interactive gradients) could give a smaller structural error.
* 6 replicates per cell in S and C and 3 in F: percentages are coarse (multiples of 1/6 or 1/3), accuracy among supported replicates is based on few units at k >= 5 for random sampling (2-3 replicates), and the apparent non-monotonic behaviour at 16,000 combinations is within replicate noise.
* Truth is conditional on the replicate's supported reference configurations (40 selected evenly), not on a population-wide set; contrasts use a 0.20-wide window, so effects are not comparable across studies with different windows.
* Balanced training sets were not reweighted; the held-out prediction target is random combinations of independent sites.
* Support is a conservative extrapolation diagnostic (per-predictor cells and a nearest-neighbour check in raw position/spread/distance space), not a proof of support in the full spline feature space (as in Figure 6).
