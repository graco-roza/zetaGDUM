# Phase II methods

Phase I remains available through `load.R` and commit `7ac8fd6`. Phase II uses `phase2_load.R`. Both are experimental modules within the repository.

## Rectangular zeta

Let $i,t,s,v$ index sites, chronological surveys, species and environmental predictors. $I,J$ are independently identifiable sets of sites and surveys, with $k=|I|$ and $m=|J|$. A balanced panel uses the same sites and species pool, comparable recording effort and perfect detection. For observed abundance $A_{its}$, incidence is $X_{its}=1(A_{its}>0)$ and relative abundance $Q_{its}=A_{its}/\sum_s A_{its}$. Local dominance $D_{its}$ is the native `dominance_matrix(A, p=0.8)` indicator computed on each original assemblage. It follows the native ordering of ties. [Hui and McGeoch (2014)](https://doi.org/10.1086/678125) · [Latombe et al. (2017)](https://doi.org/10.1111/2041-210X.12756) · [McGeoch et al. (2019)](https://doi.org/10.1002/ecy.2832)

For a rectangle, the presence sum uses $H_s=\prod_{i\in I,t\in J} X_{its}$, abundance uses $M_s=\min_{i\in I,t\in J} Q_{its}$, and dominance uses the product of $D_{its}$. Average over equally weighted site sets and either all survey sets or all windows of consecutive surveys. The output reports the range and mean of chronological spans at each order. Equal numbers of surveys can cover different spans in irregular designs. Retention divides by the previous order along the selected axis when that denominator is positive.

For incidence and dominance, first take the temporal product at each site. If $o_s(J)$ sites retain a species, the exact spatial expectation is $\binom{o_s(J)}{k}/\binom{n}{k}$. For abundance, take the minimum of original relative abundances within each site over $J$, and sort these minima across sites. The expected minimum is
$$
a_s(k\mid J)=\sum_{j=1}^{n-k+1} B_{(j)s}(J)\exp\{\operatorname{lchoose}(n-j,k-1)-\operatorname{lchoose}(n,k)\}.
$$
The weights count selections having a given smallest rank, including when several ranks tie. Temporal minima receive no second normalization. Average $a_s(k\mid J)$ and $P_s(k\mid J)$ over the same temporal sets. Then $\mu_s=a_s/P_s$ measures conditional minimum abundance for a shared species. For zero sharing $\mu_s$ is undefined and the abundance contribution is zero when relative abundance is defined. Normalise each contribution vector by its sum to obtain species weights, using zero weights when the sum is zero. The default errors when any included assemblage has total abundance zero, like the native abundance curve. The explicit `empty="undefined"` option gives an undefined abundance mean and retains the incidence and dominance outputs on the same panel.

## Directional and occupancy accounting

For fixed $I$ and endpoints $t<u$, use endpoint products $H_0,H_1$. The species ledger records $P=H_0H_1$, $G=(1-H_0)H_1$, $L=H_0(1-H_1)$. Summing gives $Z_0=P+L$, $Z_1=P+G$, $\Delta Z=G-L$. Divide gains and losses by $2P+G+L$ for directional Sørensen components, with undefined components when the denominator is zero. The same state ledger applies to dominance. Each dominance event is split according to whether species were shared by incidence at both endpoints. [Legendre (2019)](https://doi.org/10.1002/ece3.4984)

For abundance use endpoint relative minima $M_0,M_1$. The persistent amount is $\min(M_0,M_1)$, gain is $[M_1-M_0]_+$ and loss is $[M_0-M_1]_+$. Occurrence gain and loss are $G M_1$ and $L M_0$. Numerical gain and loss multiply the signed positive and negative parts by $P$. Their net sum gives the change in shared relative abundance. These are accounting quantities conditional on the observed communities.

For each species, $r_1=r_0+g-l$, with $g,l$ counting sites gained and lost. Expansion is $[g-l]_+$, contraction is $[l-g]_+$, and balanced redistribution is $\min(g,l)$. Therefore $g+l$ equals expansion plus contraction plus twice balanced redistribution. Pure redistribution requires $g=l>0$. This describes sampled locations and does not identify movements between them. Regional appearance/disappearance is determined on the complete panel. For complete spatial combination sampling, before, after and persistent contribution are $q_k(o_0),q_k(o_1),q_k(h)$ with $q_k(o)=\binom{o}{k}/\binom{n}{k}$. Subtract $q_k(h)$ from the endpoint contribution to recover species gains and losses of shared occupancy.

## Environmental design and risks

The native training-only standardization and I-spline basis are reused for every evaluation order and split in a panel. For a predictor $v$, $P_v=\sum_i x_{iv}$ and the spread column $S_{vb}$ is the mean over site pairs of $|B_{vb}(x_{iv})-B_{vb}(x_{jv})|$. Window environmental values are site-wise means before spline evaluation. Temporal changes are final minus initial. Within a set the initial and change deviations are $d_{iv}=x_{iv,0}-P_v/k$ and $\Delta d_{iv}=\Delta x_{iv}-\Delta P_v/k$. Lag is the elapsed time between window midpoints. For $m>1$, transitions use non-overlapping windows with matching within-window elapsed time vectors. Window incidence is the product of detections across the surveys. Event interpretations refer to persistent occupancy within a window. The default enumerates adjacent pairs of windows and accepts explicit starts when suitable windows are separated. [Hernández-Carrasco et al. (2026)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12920273/)

M1 uses separate Bernoulli responses for gain among initially absent site–species records and loss among initially present ones. With $z$ the initial local incidence, $c=(r_0-z)/(k-1)$, the fraction of occupied other sites. Each event has its own unconstrained intercept and coefficients on $c$, initial and changed position, initial and changed deviations, initial and changed I-spline spread, and log lag. Constant lag is omitted. At order one, deviations, spread and $c$ are structurally absent. The optional interaction is $P_v\Delta P_v$, retaining both main effects. Initial, final and change columns are linearly dependent, so the fitted specification uses initial and change. Position uses native sums. Predictions across orders are compared at equivalent changes in mean site environment, and fully specified contrasts are preferred to comparisons of coefficient magnitudes.

The composite Bernoulli objective sums eligible event terms across all sets, sites, species and transitions. Covariate-identical site–species records are aggregated exactly by event risk and initial occupancy of other sites. Rank and singular values are evaluated before fitting. A small ridge reuses the Phase I stabilizer. Separate-order prediction preserves within-rectangle identities. Known draw probabilities $q(I)$ from a with-replacement proposal can be supplied. The objective weights each sampled set by inverse $q(I)$ relative to their sample mean, targeting the uniform set distribution; this self-normalisation and the ridge remain finite-sample choices. Inference requires place/time resampling and simulated reference distributions.

Let $p_i=z_i(1-\epsilon_i)+(1-z_i)\gamma_i$. Conditional independence within a set gives $p_H=\prod_i p_i$, so $E[G_s]=(1-H_0)p_H$, $E[L_s]=H_0(1-p_H)$ and $E[Z_1]=Z_0+E[G]-E[L]$. For expansion, convolve each absent-site Bernoulli PMF into the gain-count distribution and each initially present-site Bernoulli PMF into the loss-count distribution. Their outer product is the joint working mass. Sum above or below the equal-count diagonal for expansion and contraction, sum the positive diagonal for pure redistribution, and weight all cells by $\min(g,l)$ for expected balanced redistribution. The predictions are conditional on fitted probabilities and the initial community.

M0 retains pooled shared/unshared Bernoulli fits with set-level environment and the fixed species pool. M2 fits a site-level Bernoulli gain and loss GLMM with Gaussian species intercepts, initial position, change and log lag where supported. A sensitivity fit includes mean absolute environmental gaps to a fixed set of neighbours and their changes. M2 forecasts recursively generate site × survey × species communities. The M1 overlap diagnostic compares probabilities for the same site–species–window record across sets. Its nesting diagnostic compares probabilities for shared membership in nested sets and windows under the corresponding prediction conditioning. M2's trajectories satisfy nesting within each trajectory on a common forecast origin. Contrasts conditioned on different observed window histories need not be marginals of the same conditional distribution.

Positive abundance uses Gaussian errors on log absolute abundances in separate new and persistent fits. Persistent rows include log initial positive abundance. Hierarchical sensitivity uses a Gaussian species intercept. For integer counts, the optional count fit uses a zero-truncated NB2 model, with link-scale parameters and sampling by the truncated inverse CDF. Draw final occupancy first and positive absolute abundance second, with zeros for absences. Relative abundance and native dominance are then calculated from all species in the simulated assemblage. Dissimilarities and zeta are evaluated on the draws. For higher temporal orders, relative abundance and dominance zeta are computed from M2 survey trajectories. Inserting mean abundance into a species minimum generally changes the estimand.

## Spatial differentiation

Spatial uniqueness uses native `ugdm_response()` definitions for total, turnover, nestedness, relative, balanced, Bray–Curtis and gradient dissimilarities, on their defined support. Pairwise incidence agrees with Baselga. At larger orders the native full-intersection formulation and Baselga's established multisite partition are distinct comparators. For a before/after pair of communities $A,B$, the two event orders average the change from $A$ to $A\vee B$ or $A\wedge B$, and from those counterfactuals to $B$. The signed gain and loss allocations add to $F(B)-F(A)$. The elementwise extrema apply to raw abundance, so the native relative response normalizes each counterfactual separately. Positive and negative contributions are retained separately. The published `ecopart::ecopart.pair()` provides a separate four- or six-component comparison at order two for Sørensen and Bray–Curtis. Its degenerate denominators remain visible. [Baselga (2010)](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1466-8238.2009.00490.x) · [Baselga (2013)](https://doi.org/10.1111/2041-210X.12029) · [Tatsumi et al. (2021)](https://doi.org/10.1111/ele.13720) · [Tatsumi et al. (2022)](https://doi.org/10.1111/2041-210X.13921)
