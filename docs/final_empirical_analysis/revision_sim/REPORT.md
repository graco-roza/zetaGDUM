# Simulations of the MS-GDUM spread-by-position interaction

Branch `final-empirical-analysis`; scripts in this directory, run order in `run_all.sh` (from `final/`, `ZG_LIB6` = zetaGDUM f7e115f, unmodified). The empirical analyses and the manuscript are untouched. Figure: `figures/Figure_simulation_interaction.*` (A niches and dominance, B known/independent surfaces, C errors of the fitted surfaces, D predictive gain); plotted values in `figures/*_plotted_*.csv`; all numbers below come from `tables/`.

## Design
* **Framework.** Single-stage fractional-logit MS-GDUM (`ugdm_design`, `ugdm_interaction_design`, `ugdm_fit_interactions`), as in the empirical Figure 5 fits. Three models with identical I-spline bases (built from the training sites only), logit link, nonnegativity constraints, penalties and sampled sets: **spread-only**; **additive** (spread + nonlinear cubic position, position-independent spread); **interaction** (explicit endpoint spread curves interpolated by set position). Penalties as in the empirical fits (spread 0.002, position 0.002, ridge 1e-4). Endpoint-difference shrinkage is the penalty that governs the interaction: results are reported for the empirical default 5e-3 and for weak shrinkage 1e-4 and 1e-6 (declared before the runs; the weak setting is plotted solid in panel D, the default dashed).
* **Replication and validation.** 50 replicates per scenario, 500 sites, random site-level 70/30 split, 3,000 training sets drawn from training sites and 3,000 test sets from test sites, orders 2, 3, 4, 5, 8. Environmental support is identical for all models (cells defined by the training sets). Replicates are never selected on results.
* **Conditional contrasts.** Matched profiles (k sites at position m, spread h) on a grid of 7 positions x 6 spreads (standardised gradient units); a cell is supported if >= 30 training sets lie within 0.25 SD of position and 0.25 SD of spread and all profile values lie within the observed range. The difference-in-differences contrast compares the effect of increasing spread (shortest to longest spread supported at both positions) at position -1 and +1 SD, on the link scale and on the response scale. The *explicit-interaction part* is the contrast minus the same contrast with the two endpoint curves replaced by their average (isolating the interaction from the position-dependent slope of the nonlinear I-splines that the additive model already carries). Matched support (>= 2 spreads over >= 0.8 SD at both positions) is available in all 50 replicates only at k = 2 (Sim. B: 50/50; k >= 3: none) and in 49-50 of 50 replicates at k = 2-3 (Sim. A; k = 4: 30-34, k = 5: 5-11, k = 8: none); contrasts are summarised only where it holds.

## Simulation A: recovery from known parameters (`10_simA.R`; `tables/simA_*`)
Responses ~ Binomial(60 trials, logistic(known linear predictor))/60 with focal predictor P (endpoint spread-curve heights, sums L -> U: none 2.5 -> 2.5; moderate 3.2 -> 1.6; strong 4.6 -> 0.35; interaction coefficient sum delta = U - L = 0, -1.6, -4.25) and an independent nuisance predictor N without interaction. A low-noise run (T = 1,000 trials, `*_T1000.csv`) is a sensitivity analysis.
* **Predictive performance.** Held-out D2 is within 0.001-0.008 of the noise ceiling (oracle D2 with the true probabilities) for additive models (e.g. strong, k = 2: 0.744 vs oracle 0.748). Spread-only is clearly worse than additive in every scenario (+0.005 to +0.021 for additive; positive in 47-50 of 50 replicates), including the null.
* **Paired gain of the explicit interaction over additive (weak shrinkage 1e-6; median [95% interval], positive of 50):** strong k = 2: +0.0010 [0.0000, 0.0049] 48/50; k = 3: +0.0030 [0.0004, 0.0081] 49/50; k = 4: +0.0016 (44/50); k = 5: +0.0007 (32/50); k = 8: -0.0010 (11/50). Moderate: +0.0003 (39/50) at k = 2, +0.0003 (30/50) at k = 3, negative from k = 4. **Null: -0.00005 to -0.0015 at every order (3-21 of 50 positive).** With the empirical default shrinkage (5e-3) the interaction never improves held-out D2 (median -0.0003 to -0.0015; <= 8/50 positive). With T = 1,000 the strong-scenario gains are 0.0013-0.0052 (49-50/50 at k = 2-4), still small. Gains vanish or turn negative by k = 5-8, where matched support is lost and the interaction coefficients are poorly identified.
* **Recovery of the known interaction coefficient (delta_P_sum, `simA_summary_coefficient_recovery.csv`).** *Unsuccessful with the default shrinkage*: the estimate is essentially zero in all scenarios (strong k = 2: -0.05 against -4.25). With weak shrinkage (1e-6): none -0.96 [-1.31, -0.47] (truth 0), moderate -1.73 [-1.99, -1.25] (truth -1.6), strong -2.98 [-3.64, -2.36] (truth -4.25) at k = 2. The scenarios are ordered correctly, but the null is **not** recovered as zero: the weak-shrinkage model reports a spurious interaction of the same sign as the true effect, and the nuisance predictor N (truth 0) receives -0.71 [-0.97, -0.37] at k = 2. Coefficient recovery deteriorates with order (strong, k = 5: -1.16 against -4.25). The estimated coefficients therefore cannot be read as a calibrated interaction size; the weak-shrinkage estimate is biased toward spurious interaction and the default estimate toward none.
* **Conditional response surfaces.** At k = 2 the interaction (1e-6) has the smallest surface error in the moderate and strong scenarios (link RMSE 0.016 strong vs additive 0.020, spread-only 0.055) and is marginally worse than additive under the null (0.0115 vs 0.0110). At k = 5 the additive model is better under the null (link RMSE 0.017 vs 0.026) and the moderate scenario (0.024 vs 0.028) and comparable in the strong scenario (0.035 vs 0.034 for 1e-6; 0.039 for the default).
* **Contrasts (link and response scale).** The total conditional contrast is largely carried by the nonlinear I-splines: the additive model recovers 80% of the strong k = 2 link contrast (-0.383 vs true -0.480; response scale -0.091 vs -0.113). Explicit-interaction part, strong k = 2: true -0.295 (link), -0.075 (response); estimated -0.219 / -0.055 with weak shrinkage and -0.004 / -0.001 with the default. Under the null the estimated explicit part is -0.068 (link) / -0.017 (response) with weak shrinkage (spurious; about a third of the strong-scenario estimate), -0.001 with the default. In the strong scenario the explicit part is recovered at 74% (k = 2), 58% (k = 3), 45% (k = 4) and 26% (k = 5, 11 replicates with matched support), i.e. recovery decays with order.

## Simulation B: ecological mechanism (`11a_simB_truth.R`, `11b_simB_fit.R`; `tables/simB_*`)
* **Generator.** 500 sites on a productivity gradient (U(0,1)), 200 taxa: 120 specialists (Gaussian niches, SD 0.07) and 80 generalists (SD 0.35), within-guild abundances lognormal; local relative abundance = guild share x niche-weighted within-guild share. Generalist dominance 0.1 + 0.8 x logistic(10(x - 0.5)) (logistic scenario) or 0.5 (control). Counts: multinomial with a constant 2,000 individuals per site. An independent nuisance predictor N does not influence the communities. Responses: incidence (Sorensen-type), relative abundance, occupancy-associated and heterogeneity components.
* **Independent truth.** For every matched profile the Monte Carlo mean (300 multinomial replicates) of the package response functions on simulated counts; no fitted model is used (`simB_truth_surfaces.csv`).
* **Information criteria.** Sharing probability is ~1 at all five orders (>= 0.10 in all 50 replicates), so every order passes the compositional criterion; environmental support limits the matched contrasts to k = 2 (all replicates) and gives 11-26 supported cells at k = 2-5 and 4 at k = 8.
* **Position (additive vs spread-only).** Held-out D2 gain of nonlinear position over spread-only, relative abundance, logistic: +0.033 (k = 2), +0.059, +0.075, +0.088, **+0.122** (k = 8), all 50/50 positive; control: +0.018 (k = 2) falling to about 0 at k >= 4. Position is therefore detected at all orders under the logistic dominance shift and is largest at the highest order.
* **Explicit interaction beyond additive (weak shrinkage, median [95%], positive of 50).** Relative abundance: logistic +0.012 (k = 2; 50/50), +0.0016 (k = 3), +0.0115 (k = 4), **+0.025 (k = 5; 50/50)**, +0.015 (k = 8); control **+0.031 (k = 2; 50/50)**, +0.0066 (k = 3), -0.0009, -0.0003, -0.0042 (k = 8). Incidence: logistic +0.044, +0.021, +0.003, +0.0003, +0.010; control +0.048, +0.037, +0.012, +0.003, -0.0005. With the default shrinkage (5e-3) gains are <= 0.008 and mostly negative from k = 4.
  - At k = 2 the explicit interaction improves held-out D2 even in the **control**, where dominance is constant, so the low-order gain is not caused by the dominance shift (it reflects niche structure and the extra flexibility of the varying-spread model). At k >= 4 gains for relative abundance occur only in the logistic scenario (+0.012 to +0.025 vs control <= 0), which is the pattern expected if a position-dependent spread effect is generated by the dominance shift.
* **Explicit test of whether the ecology generates an interaction beyond additive I-splines (`14_projection_test.R`, `projection_test.csv`).** The Monte Carlo (or known) surface is refitted on supported cells by the three model forms. Interaction-to-additive RMSE ratio (1 = no benefit): Simulation A null 1.06-1.29 (no benefit, as designed), moderate 0.85-1.03, strong 0.76-0.91; Simulation B logistic 0.92-1.03 (relative abundance 0.965-1.03; occupancy 0.91-0.96; incidence 0.92-0.97; heterogeneity 0.98-1.00), control 0.92-1.07 (projection available for k = 2-5 in Sim. B; fewer than 12 supported cells at k = 8). **The simulated ecology therefore generates, at most, a small explicit interaction beyond what the additive nonlinear I-splines represent (about 3-9% surface-error reduction, less than the strong known-parameter case); for relative abundance at k >= 3 the surface itself shows no benefit although held-out gains exist.** This mismatch (gains in prediction that the surface projection does not explain) is unresolved; extra flexibility of the varying-spread model on this out-of-family generator is a plausible but untested explanation. Heterogeneity is projected perfectly by the additive form.
* **Surface recovery (`simB_summary_surface.csv`).** Spread-only is worst for the logistic relative-abundance surface (response RMSE 0.072 at k = 2, 0.091 at k = 5) against additive 0.051/0.052 and interaction 0.048/0.046 (weak shrinkage); the interaction improves modestly over additive for incidence at k = 2 (0.045 vs 0.049) and not at k = 5.
* **Contrast recovery: partly unsuccessful (k = 2, `simB_summary_contrasts.csv`).** Relative abundance (logistic): true link contrast -0.76, additive -0.65, interaction -0.83 (response scale -0.096 vs -0.126 and -0.144): the sign and rough size are recovered by both models. Control relative abundance: true -0.034 but estimated +0.011 (additive) / +0.014 (interaction): wrong sign. **Incidence: true contrast +0.144 (logistic) and +0.058 (control) but estimated -0.146/-0.147 and -0.014/-0.011: wrong sign in both scenarios.** Occupancy (logistic) true -1.77, estimated -0.72/-0.85. **Heterogeneity (logistic) true +2.41, estimated +0.33/+0.48: strongly underestimated.** The link-scale truth for components near 0 or 1 is extreme and weakly determined; these contrasts should not be interpreted.
* **Convergence.** 5 of 2,000 spread-only fits (Sim. B) and 1 of 750 (Sim. A) did not converge; none removed; all additive and interaction fits converged.

## Summary of what is and is not supported
* With known parameters the explicit interaction is identifiable only at low orders (k = 2-3) and then yields small predictive gains (<= 0.005 D2); under the empirical default shrinkage it is effectively never recovered and never improves held-out D2, and under weak shrinkage it is recovered with a spurious interaction in the null and in a nuisance predictor.
* In the ecological simulation the main predictive value comes from nonlinear position (guild dominance), not from the explicit interaction. The explicit interaction adds small gains that appear at k = 2 irrespective of dominance and at k = 4-8 only under the dominance shift; the surface projection indicates that the generated surface is mostly representable by additive I-splines.
* Negative/inconclusive results above (null spurious recovery, wrong-sign incidence contrasts, heterogeneity contrasts, loss of matched support beyond k = 3-5) are reported as obtained.

## Limitations
* A single replicate design per scenario; one nuisance predictor; response noise (T = 60) chosen a priori; the shrinkage levels are a sensitivity range, not tuned.
* Matched contrasts rely on declared support windows; beyond k = 3-5 they are unavailable, so the order dependence of interaction recovery rests on a shrinking subset of replicates.
* Held-out D2 differences of the order 1e-3 are statistically consistent but small relative to the noise ceiling; Wilcoxon and sign-test p-values in `*_summary_gains.csv` are descriptive and uncorrected (the 50 replicates are independent simulations, but the three interaction settings and five orders are tested without correction).
* The surface truth in Simulation B is a Monte Carlo mean with the standard error given in `simB_truth_surfaces.csv` (`mc_se_*`); the link-scale truth for components near 0 or 1 is unstable.
