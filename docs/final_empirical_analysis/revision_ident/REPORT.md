# Decisive validation of the MS-GDUM interaction implementation

Branch `final-empirical-analysis`; scripts here (order in `run_all.sh`; run from `final/` with `ZG_LIB6` = zetaGDUM f7e115f). The public package API, the empirical analyses, the earlier simulations (`../revision_sim`) and the manuscript are unchanged: all new model variants (focal-restricted, cross-predictor, matched penalties) are built in `00_ident_common.R` from the package's own design pieces, and the builder reproduces the package's `.interaction_layout()` exactly (maximum difference 0) and its penalty exactly when the doubled ridge is not corrected. Figure: `figures/Figure_decisive_interaction_validation.*`; all numbers come from `tables/`.

## Decision (stated first)
1. **Same-predictor interaction (current `spread = "varying"`): not identifiable at k = 2 and only weakly identifiable at k >= 3; not useful in the ecological simulation.** At k = 2 it is exactly rank-deficient; at k >= 3 only 1-3% of the interaction columns' variance lies outside the additive design; the default shrinkage removes any identifiable interaction; and it gave no recovery of the simulated productivity-dependent pH effect while producing a spurious predictive gain under constant dominance.
2. **A reduced or reparameterised interaction is necessary.** The interaction should be re-expressed in coordinates orthogonal to the additive design (or restricted to the identifiable directions), with the doubled ridge corrected and shrinkage defined on the identifiable component.
3. **The cross-predictor interaction is the more defensible ecological extension.** It is full rank at every order including k = 2, recovered the sign of the productivity-dependent pH effect in 100% of replicates in both non-null scenarios, and was silent under constant dominance, where the additive and same-predictor models cannot see the effect at all.
4. **Explicit interactions should not be removed outright, but the current same-predictor implementation should be withdrawn from default use** (deprecate or hide behind an experimental, rank-checked interface) and replaced by the reparameterised cross-predictor form. This is a recommendation; the API was not changed. Caveat: the cross-predictor result is from a generator in which such an interaction was declared in advance; it is evidence of identifiability and of what the model can detect, not evidence that the effect exists in the EPA data (where the earlier analyses found no benefit from the same-predictor form).

## A. Mathematical identifiability (`20_identifiability_A.R`; `tables/identifiability_A_*`)
Random, balanced and EPA (macroinvertebrates, shared pool) combinations; k = 2, 3, 4, 5, 8; package parameterisation (all predictors) and focal-only.
* **k = 2: exact rank deficiency, one per interaction predictor.** Full design rank 17 of 19 (all predictors) and 15 of 16 (focal-only), singular-value ratio 1e-16 (scaled condition 1e15) in all three data sources. The null vector is 0.5 f2(1-u) + f3(1-u) - f1 u - 0.5 f2 u = 0 (columns `P_f2_L`, `P_f3_L`, `P_f1_U`, `P_f2_U`). Algebra: the three degree-2 I-splines (no interior knot) are cubic polynomials in the rescaled predictor t, so for a pair with mean position u = (t_a + t_b)/2 and spread s = |t_a - t_b|, the spread columns are s(alpha + 2 beta u + 3 gamma (u^2 + s^2/12)); they span {s, su, s(u^2 + s^2/12)}. The interaction columns add {su^2, su(u^2 + s^2/12)} but "set position times linear spread" (s u) is already in the additive span, so six endpoint columns span only five functions. This is the dependency the task anticipated (u s = |t_a^2 - t_b^2|/2); the package's own `ugdm_interaction_diagnostics()` reports rank 17 of 19 and infinite condition at k = 2 too.
* **k >= 3: full numerical rank but near-redundant.** Scaled condition 2e2-9e2 (package, weighted: 5e4-4e5). The U-endpoint (u x spread) columns are 97-99.9% explained by the additive design (mean residual fraction: random sets 0.31% at k = 2, 1.0, 0.97, 0.90, 0.59% at k = 3, 4, 5, 8; balanced 0.15, 1.3, 1.7, 1.9, 1.7%; EPA 0.34, 1.2, 1.1, 0.9, 0.6%; focal-only balanced up to 3.0%). Several residual dimensions are numerically non-zero (4-8 singular values above 10% of the largest, of 6-9 interaction columns), but together they carry only about 1-3% of the variance. No exact dependency exists at k >= 3 (algebra above needs the pair identity), so similar dependencies are approximate, not exact, at higher orders.

## B. Balanced simulation with known parameters (`01_balanced_design.R`, `21_balanced_known.R`; `tables/balancedB_*`)
* **Design.** Fixed reference basis; independent training and test configurations; 42 position x spread cells x 30 configurations (arrangements: symmetric, outlier, two clusters, uniform, skewed; k = 2: pairs); random sets as comparison arm. Scenarios: none; moderate and strong identifiable (outside-additive SD 0.15 and 0.40 logit units; at k = 2 achievable only to 0.05 and 0.10 within effect-size caps of 2 and 4 logit units, because the identifiable part is about 2.5% of the interaction); redundant (smallest outside-additive content: exactly 0 at k = 2, SD 0.05 at k >= 3; `balancedB_truth_parameters_info.csv`). Endpoint coefficients nonnegative. Exact (no noise) and noisy (Binomial 60, 50 replicates) experiments.
* **Fit sequence.** Unpenalised; ridge; + curvature; + interaction shrinkage (5e-3 and 1e-6); penalties selected by inner validation (random 70/30 split of the training configurations; spread/position smoothing 2e-4-2e-2 x shrinkage 5e-3/1e-4/1e-6). Additive and equal-endpoint interaction models have identical penalties: the interaction penalty subtracts ridge/2 on the endpoint columns, so the equal-endpoint penalty equals the additive one exactly (check: 0.0100406 = 0.0100406; the package's unmodified penalty gave 0.010293).
* **Optimiser agreement.** Package optimiser vs independent constrained optimiser (nlminb, unscaled, analytic gradient) on the identical objective: median maximum coefficient difference 1e-7, prediction difference 2e-7 (95th percentile <= 2e-6), objective difference 1e-14, in every penalised fit. The package optimiser is therefore correct; disagreement only appears where the model is rank-deficient (k = 2 unpenalised) and then only in non-identifiable directions.
* **Exact data, unpenalised (no noise): the interaction is recoverable at k >= 3.** Outside-additive component correlation 1.000, link-scale explicit contrast recovered (strong k = 3: 1.773 estimated vs 1.773 true; k = 5: 1.578 vs 1.578), prediction error vs truth 2e-5 - 2e-4. **At k = 2** the identifiable component is recovered (correlation 1.0) but the explicit contrast is wrong (strong: +0.124 vs -0.064, wrong sign) because it includes the unidentifiable direction. The redundant control's outside-additive component is exactly zero at k = 2 and small (SD 0.05) at k >= 3, where it is recovered.
* **Noisy data (50 replicates), unpenalised, balanced configurations.** Explicit link contrast (strong): 1.65 [1.32, 1.96] vs 1.77 (k = 3), 1.57 vs 1.58 (k = 5); paired held-out D2 gain over the unpenalised additive model strong +0.0041 (k = 3), +0.0099 (k = 5), +0.0123 (k = 8), moderate +0.0076, +0.0123, +0.0128, all 50/50 positive; k = 2: +0.00005 (strong, 32/50). Under the null the median gain is -0.0004 to -0.0007 (13-16/50 positive), the outside-additive SD of the estimated interaction 0.0085 (95th percentile 0.016, truth 0) and the explicit contrast 0.002 [-0.064, 0.053]; redundant control: gain +0.0001-0.0003 (essentially zero). Random combinations gave smaller gains (strong +0.0025 k = 3, +0.0041 k = 5; contrasts 1.68 and 1.45 vs 1.77 and 1.58).
* **Regularisation destroys recovery at these effect sizes.** With the penalties as implemented, even exact data give explicit contrast 4e-4 (shrink 5e-3) and 0.067 (shrink 1e-6) against 1.77 (strong k = 3). Noisy strong gains fall to +0.00001 (default shrink), +0.0012 (1e-6), +0.0007 (jointly selected; inner validation chose weak shrinkage in 100% of strong and moderate replicates but could not recover the effect). The identifiable direction needs large coefficients (small information per unit coefficient), which any ridge/shrinkage penalises heavily. Under the null the jointly selected models are quiet (median gain |x| <= 1e-4).
* **Coefficients.** The interaction coefficient sum is recovered only for full-rank unpenalised fits (strong k = 5: 48.2 vs 48.5; RMSE of the three endpoint differences 3.3 (strong), 1.5 (moderate)); rank-deficient (k = 2) and penalised fits are judged only by predictions and the functional contrasts above.

## C and D. Ecological simulation with independent truth (`30_meta_common.R`, `31_meta_truth.R`, `32_meta_fit.R`; `tables/meta_*`)
* **Generator (declared before evaluation, not tuned).** 625 sites on a 25 x 25 pH x productivity grid (jittered, redrawn per replicate), 200 species (120 specialists, pH niche SD 0.08; 80 generalists, SD 0.35; optima spread across pH), expected abundance = guild dominance(P) x Gaussian pH response x species parameter, normalised, multinomial with 2,000 individuals per site; communities generated per site, never per combination. Scenarios: constant dominance; generalists increase with P; reversed.
* **Independent truth.** Monte Carlo mean (200 communities) of the package response functions for matched pH-profile configurations (3 pH positions x 3 spreads x 3 productivity levels x 5 internal arrangements). Truth behaves as designed (relative abundance, pH position 0.5): pH-spread effect (spread 0.1 -> 0.3) is +0.30 at every productivity under constant dominance, +0.41 at P = 0.2 falling to +0.18 at P = 0.8 when generalists increase, and reversed in the reversed scenario (difference-in-differences, link scale: -1.12 increasing, +1.11 reversed, -0.004 constant at k = 2).
* **Models** (matched penalties; interaction restricted to the focal predictor pH): spread-only; additive nonlinear; same-predictor (pH spread interpolated by pH position); cross-predictor (pH spread interpolated by productivity position; same endpoint architecture; scripts only). Balanced sampling (75 cells of mean pH x pH spread x mean productivity, several arrangements, nearest real sites; 64-69 of 75 cells filled at k = 2-5) and random sampling; 50 replicates, random 70/30 site split.
* **Rank/support.** Spread-only 7, additive 13 columns (full rank); same-predictor 15 of 16 at k = 2 (condition 9e14) and 16 of 16 at k >= 3; **cross-predictor 16 of 16 at every order including k = 2** (condition 41-56).
* **Predictive gains (relative abundance, balanced, weak shrinkage 1e-6; median [95%], positive of 50).** Additive over spread-only: +0.07 to +0.25 under dominance gradients (incidence), ~0 under constant dominance. Cross over additive: increasing +0.038 [0.028, 0.053] (k = 2), +0.023, +0.015, +0.010, +0.0045 (k = 8), 50/50 at every order; reversed +0.036 ... +0.0047; **constant -0.0002 to -0.00005, 15-22/50 positive**. With the default shrinkage the gains shrink to +0.007 (k = 2) and +0.0003 (k = 8) in the non-null scenarios and 0.000004 under the null. Same over additive: +0.0015 (increasing), +0.0015 (reversed), **+0.0037 (constant) at k = 2, +0.0056 at k = 3**: the same-predictor model gains as much or more under constant dominance (a spurious gain; random sampling: +0.030 at k = 2 under constant dominance vs +0.009 under dominance gradients). Cross over same: about +0.037 (k = 2) under dominance gradients.
* **Conditional contrasts (productivity-dependent pH effect, high minus low P, link scale, k = 2; truth -1.12 / +1.11 / -0.004).** Additive 0 and same-predictor 0 (cannot represent it); cross-predictor -0.77 [-0.87, -0.71] (69%) and +0.78 [0.69, 0.90] with weak shrinkage, -0.08 and +0.08 with the default shrinkage (7%); sign correct in 100% of replicates; RMSE 0.34 (weak) vs 1.04-1.12 (default / additive). Response scale: truth -0.228 / +0.227; cross -0.169 / +0.170 (weak), additive -0.024 / +0.023. At k = 3 and 5 the cross model recovers 54% (k = 3) and 58% (k = 5) of the link contrast; the additive model at k = 3 and 5 estimates the response-scale contrast with the wrong sign at k = 3 (+0.082 vs -0.084) and a wrong magnitude at k = 5. Under constant dominance the cross-predictor contrast is -0.005 [-0.06, +0.05] at k = 2 (weak shrinkage; wide but centred on 0; sign correct in 52% by chance) and -0.0007 with the default shrinkage. Outside-additive SD of the estimated interaction under the null: 0.008 (cross, k = 2; 95th percentile 0.040), 0.003 (same), against 0.12 for the cross model in the dominance scenarios.
* **Surface error and fit.** Held-out D2 (relative abundance, balanced, increasing dominance): additive 0.921, same 0.922 (k = 2); cross 0.959 (weak) / 0.928 (default); response-surface RMSE 0.060 (additive, same) vs 0.039 (cross, weak) at k = 2; at k = 5 0.0365 vs 0.0329. Incidence: cross gain +0.0027 at k = 2 and +0.035 at k = 5 under dominance gradients, ~0 under constant.
* **Failures and limits.** The same-predictor contrast of pH position (secondary, `link_did_X`) is not recovered by any model (truth -0.03 to -0.04, RMSE 0.03); the cross-predictor model recovers only 54-69% of the dominance contrast even with weak shrinkage; with the default shrinkage the cross contrast is under-recovered by >90%. 30 of 36,000 spread-only fits did not converge (none removed); all additive and interaction fits converged.

## E. Summary of evaluation items
Held-out D2 and paired gains (`balancedB_summary_gains.csv`, `meta_summary_gains.csv`); surface RMSE and contrast bias/RMSE (`meta_summary_contrasts.csv`, `meta_summary_performance.csv`); false recovery under the null (`balancedB_summary_interaction_recovery.csv`, `meta_summary_outside_additive.csv`, `meta_summary_contrasts.csv`); coefficient recovery for identifiable parameters (`balancedB_summary_interaction_recovery.csv`); rank, condition and design support (`identifiability_A_*`, `balancedB_summary_rank.csv`, `meta_summary_performance.csv`); regularisation sensitivity (fit-stage tables); balanced vs random sampling (all tables have an `arm` column).

## Limitations
* One declared generator and one known-parameter family; cross-predictor performance depends on the existence of the simulated interaction. No empirical test of the cross-predictor model was made.
* Identifiable-interaction effect sizes were capped (2 and 4 logit units total contribution) and, at k = 2, the declared outside-additive variation could not be reached; strong/moderate scenarios therefore differ less at k = 2.
* The interaction increment's identifiability depends on the sampled design; balanced configurations improved recovery relative to random ones but did not change the k = 2 algebra.
* Descriptive p-values (`wilcoxon_p`) are uncorrected; intervals are central 95% over independent replicates.
* "Selected" penalties use a small grid and a single inner split.
