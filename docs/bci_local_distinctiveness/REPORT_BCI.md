# Report: what local multisite metrics add to LCBD at BCI

Data and methods are documented in `README.md`; manuscript-ready paragraphs and captions are in `MANUSCRIPT_TEXT_BCI.md`. Main figure: `figures/Figure4_main.{pdf,svg,png}` (layout A LCBD map, B characteristic retention order map, C retention profiles, D LCBD versus T). Supporting figures `FigureS1-S6`.

## Reproduction of the published analysis
Tree-record counts (status A, dbh ≥ 10 mm) reproduce Legendre & Condit (2019): 244,015 individuals in 1990, +1,354 swamp and −35,697 non-swamp individuals 1985-2015. Hellinger LCBD equals `adespatial::beta.div` exactly. With 99,999 permutations and Holm correction, 1985 has 14 significant quadrats, 12 of them swamp; 2015 has 2, none in the swamp (126 and 26 swamp quadrats significant before correction), the pattern described in the paper. Stems instead of tree records change nothing material (LCBD rho 0.97-0.98; occurrence results identical), so stem counts are not needed. Limits: 322 versus 328 species in the 1985/2015 union; the 7-category habitat file cannot be matched to the paper's six groups (500 quadrats in group 1 is not reproducible), only the swamp (30 quadrats).

## Characteristic (T) versus conditional (T+) retention
* **T** = sum over orders of the local conditional zeta profile (expected number of orders over which a site's composition survives in sets that contain it). It is dominated by what is lost at order two, so for relative abundance it is a near-monotone function of pairwise distinctiveness (rho with LCBD = −0.86; 78% of variance explained by LCBD). What it adds: with **occurrence** data it is unrelated to LCBD (rho = −0.03).
* **T+** = 1 + (T − 1)/ζ2|i conditions on sharing at order two. It asks how persistently the part that is shared survives, independently of how little is shared. In the swamp T and T+ are decoupled (rho −0.08; −0.76 elsewhere). Distinctive quadrats retain their shared residue longer (T+ versus LCBD rho 0.84): the shared part consists of widespread dominants.
Neither is a measure of conservation value or irreplaceability.

## Findings by question
1. **Persistence across orders.** Abundance-based LCZD stays aligned with LCBD (ρ 0.93 at k=2, 0.62 at k=80, 0.53 at k=320); 93% of swamp quadrats are in the top decile at k=2 and 83% at k=80. Incidence-based LCZD loses the signal: ρ 0.44 → 0.06 (k=2 → 80), swamp in the top decile 33% → 0% at k=640. High pairwise LCBD persists across orders only through abundance.
2. **Similar LCBD, different retention.** Yes, for occurrence. LCBD explains 3% of the variance in occurrence-based T (residual SD ≈ 2 orders; plot-wide 5-95% range 6.4 orders). Examples: two swamp quadrats with LCBD 1.55 and 1.56 × 10⁻³ have T = 11.1 and 19.6; a swamp and a slope quadrat with LCBD 1.39 and 1.41 × 10⁻³ have T = 20.2 and 12.3. In the second pair the relative-abundance profiles are almost identical, so the difference lies in occupancy (which species are shared and how widely), not relative abundance. For abundance-based T the effect is small (median difference 0.08 orders among pairs with LCBD within 2%).
3. **Swamp.** Distinctive and unrepresentative in abundance (LCBD Cliff's δ = 0.97; T −0.91), but more retentive in occurrence (T δ = 0.57, T+ δ = 0.88) because swamp quadrats are species-poor (35 versus 54 species, 95 versus 193 trees) subsets of regional composition with unusual proportions. The swamp is not a distinct species pool.
4. **Abundance changes spatial identification.** LCZD relative versus incidence ρ = 0.36 (k=2) and 0.13 (k=80); top-decile overlap (Jaccard) 0.10-0.20. T (occurrence) highlights a different set of quadrats than LCBD (map B versus A).
5. **Species.** Occupancy-restricted: *Bactris major* (57 quadrats, 63% of swamp quadrats) contributes 0.095 of the swamp mean expected dissimilarity (0.71) at k=2. Dominance: *Faramea occidentalis* (all quadrats, 12% of swamp individuals versus 10% elsewhere) contributes 0.045 (k=2) and 0.105 (k=40), 99% and 86% through abundance heterogeneity. The heterogeneity share of local dissimilarity is lowest in the swamp (0.33 versus 0.38-0.57 at k=2) and falls with order in all habitats (0.15-0.28 at k=40): differentiation at high orders is increasingly occupancy. The conspicuous swamp indicator *Elaeis oleifera* (1.1% of quadrats, 37% of swamp quadrats) ranks only 23rd (0.009, entirely occupancy); the ten largest contributors account for 39% of the swamp total, so the signal is spread over many species and arises from the joint distribution of restricted occupancy and unusual dominance, not from a single indicator.
6. **Census years.** Swamp LCBD fell 1.78 → 1.33 × 10⁻³ (others unchanged); swamp quadrats in the top 5% LCBD fell from 26 to 20 of 63. Abundance-based T rose by 2.0 in the swamp (0.7-0.8 elsewhere) but stayed near the bottom of the plot (percentile 0.06 → 0.08), whereas occurrence-based T rose only 1.6 (3.5-4.7 elsewhere), cancelling its earlier above-average retention (percentile 0.78 → 0.48). Quadrat-level stability: LCBD ρ = 0.66, T (abundance) 0.63, T (occurrence) 0.39 (swamp 0.75, 0.81, 0.12). Swamp quadrats gained 11 species and 35 trees (median), other habitats lost trees.

## Informative exceptions
* 37 of the 63 top-5% LCBD quadrats are not swamp (mostly slope and low plateau). Their occurrence-based T covers almost the whole plot range (percentile 0.001-0.99). Slope quadrats x047_y017 and x043_y025 combine top-5% LCBD with the lowest occurrence-based T (percentile < 0.01): distinct and unrepresentative by every measure; quadrats x015_y014 and x017_y015 (mixed habitat) combine top-5% LCBD with T in the top 10%: distinct yet strongly retained.
* Slope quadrat x004_y023 (LCBD top 1%) has the highest conditional retention T+ (abundance) of the plot, higher than any swamp quadrat.
* Distinctiveness does not imply low retention: occurrence-based T is independent of LCBD, and T+ is positively related to it.

## Negative findings and caveats
* For relative abundance, T carries little information beyond LCBD (rho −0.86); the added value is T+, occurrence, and the order-resolved LCZD.
* Swamp quadrats hold half as many individuals as other quadrats, so part of their distinctiveness may reflect sampling variance (small samples inflate Hellinger distances); no rarefaction was applied.
* Retention orders depend on plot extent, quadrat size and the number of quadrats (n = 1250); absolute orders are not transferable, rank comparisons are.
* The habitat map has 7 categories rather than the paper's 6 groups; only the swamp and total were verified.
* Incidence local species contributions are in richness units (unnormalised), not directly comparable to relative-abundance contributions.
* Census comparison confounds real change with the overall 14.9% stem decline (Legendre & Condit); metrics are relative within a census, so T values across censuses are not on a common absolute scale.
* Species contributions use a per-species call of the compiled routine behind `conditional_zeta()` (internal function, no package change); it sums exactly to the exported LCZD (error 1e-15) for relative abundance and incidence.
