# Supplement to Figure 6: replacement-oriented components (turnover, nestedness-resultant, balanced variation, abundance gradient)

Same datasets (US macroinvertebrates 1081 lakes, US zooplankton 1093 lakes, Polish phytoplankton 117 lakes), predictors (log10 TP, TN, Chl-a + great-circle distance), five spatial blocks and informative orders as the main analysis. Package zetaGDUM at f7e115f, unmodified. Descriptive predictive attribution only; no significance testing.

## What was fitted
`ugdm_response()` components: **turnover** (incidence, 1 - shared/min richness), **nestedness-resultant** (Sørensen minus Simpson dissimilarity), **balanced variation** (abundance, raw abundances, 1 - a/min total abundance), **abundance gradient** (abundance, Bray-Curtis minus balanced). These are handled by the original/extended MS-GDUM (`ugdm_design` → `ugdm_interaction_design` → `ugdm_fit_interactions` → `ugdm_within_order_importance`), not by the coherent component architecture used for Figure 6. Three models: linear position + constant spread (original MS-GDUM baseline; there is no position-free model in this branch of the package), nonlinear position + constant spread (additive, primary), nonlinear position + position-dependent spread (explicit spread × position interaction). Fixed declared penalties (spread/position smoothing 0.002, interaction shrink 0.005, ridge 1e-4); sensitivity with interaction shrink 1e-4. 3 seeds × 5 leave-one-block-out folds × 3000 training and 3000 held-out sets (`repl/10_fit.R`). 3840 fits, 1 non-converged.

## Main results
1. **Predictability differs strongly by component.** Median held-out D2 across orders (additive model): turnover 0.16 (macroinvertebrates), 0.51 (phytoplankton), −0.01 (zooplankton); balanced variation 0.12 / 0.03 / 0.11; nestedness-resultant 0.04 / −0.02 / 0.05; abundance gradient 0.01 / −0.08 / 0.08. Negative D2 means worse than the training mean: nestedness-resultant and gradient are essentially unpredictable for phytoplankton, and turnover is unpredictable for zooplankton at most orders (4 of 5 orders ≤ 0).
2. **Component magnitude and predictor importance move independently.** For macroinvertebrates the magnitude of nestedness-resultant falls from 0.10 (k=2) to 0.024 (k=10) and abundance gradient from 0.066 to 0.012, while turnover and balanced variation approach 1 (0.95, 0.98). Yet TP's absolute held-out contribution *rises* for every component: turnover 0.020 → 0.130, nestedness-resultant 0.019 → 0.105, balanced 0.028 → 0.102, gradient ≈ 0 → 0.058 (`FigS_R4`). The small high-order nestedness/gradient signal is therefore more TP-aligned, not less. In relative terms TP's share of held-out D2 rises from 0.14 to 0.55 (turnover) and 0.36 to 0.80 (nestedness) between k=2 and 10. Relative shares > 1 (e.g. 2.3 for balanced variation at k=10) occur because TN and geographic distance contribute negatively (held-out) and total D2 is small; they should not be read literally.
3. **Zooplankton: Chl-a, not TP, carries the abundance components.** Chl-a's absolute contribution rises with order (balanced variation 0.02 → 0.13, gradient 0.04 → 0.12 at k=2 → 6) while TP stays ≤ 0.026; Chl-a correlates 0.73 with TP and responds to enrichment, so this is a predictive, not independent, signal. Total D2 changes little (0.06 → 0.07 balanced), so the increase is a reallocation among correlated predictors plus negative contributions of TN/distance, not more explained deviance.
4. **Phytoplankton: geography only.** For turnover (D2 0.4–0.5) geographic distance supplies essentially all the explained deviance (0.38 at k=2 to 0.26 at k=5), TP/TN/Chl-a ≈ 0 or negative. Balanced variation: distance 0.18 → 0.10. Nutrients explain nothing transferable in 117 lakes.
5. **Macroinvertebrates, low orders:** geographic distance leads for balanced variation (0.06 at k=2–3) and is the second contributor to turnover; at k ≥ 6 TP dominates all four components and distance turns negative.
6. **Continuous trophic position (nonlinear position).** Nonlinear versus linear position improves held-out D2 for macroinvertebrate turnover (+0.008 to +0.011 at each order) and nestedness-resultant (+0.003 to +0.009), and for zooplankton gradient (+0.001 to +0.009); gains are ≤ 0.01 elsewhere and sometimes negative (macroinvertebrate gradient −0.004 to −0.006 at k ≤ 5).
7. **Explicit spread × position interaction: not informative.** Median held-out gain over the additive model is within ±0.002 for nearly all cells (default and 1e-4 penalty; `FigS_R6`); the largest effects are harmful (macroinvertebrate balanced variation −0.018 at k=10 and −0.01 at k=8; zooplankton turnover −0.003 at k=6). The hypothesis that explicit interactions reveal extra information at multisite orders is not supported for any component or taxon here.
8. **Context dependence of TP spread effects (`FigS_R5`, order 3, supported common spread range, conditional on covarying TN, Chl-a and distance).** Macroinvertebrate turnover rises 0.077 per unit TP spread among oligotrophic (TP≈10 µg/L) lakes and 0.057 among eutrophic (≈100 µg/L) lakes; balanced variation 0.046 vs 0.031; nestedness-resultant is flat in oligotrophic (0.0007) and weakly positive in eutrophic (0.0086) lakes. At order 5 the turnover contrast reverses (0.067 vs 0.084), so the direction of context dependence is order-specific. Zooplankton balanced variation rises more steeply in eutrophic lakes (0.024 vs 0.049) while the abundance gradient declines (−0.026), opposite to its oligotrophic slope. Phytoplankton oligotrophic position is unsupported, and mesotrophic vs eutrophic slopes differ by ≤ 0.03, with D2 ≈ 0 for nestedness and gradient (not interpretable). These curves reflect the nonlinear position terms and observed cell means (circles in the figure) scatter around them; no cell contrast has an uncertainty estimate.

## Negative findings and caveats
- Nestedness-resultant and gradient are poorly predicted outside macroinvertebrates and, for gradient, zooplankton; phytoplankton nestedness, gradient and (mostly) balanced variation have negative held-out D2 at most orders.
- Directional (spread) effects in the extended fit are constrained non-negative (`ugdm_fit_interactions` has no `free_sign`); nestedness and gradient have no expected direction. Using the original `ugdm_fit` with `free_sign = TRUE` changes held-out D2 for these two components erratically (−0.25 to +0.38, no consistent sign; `tables_repl/free_sign_sensitivity.csv`), so the constraint neither clearly helps nor hurts, but the sign structure of nestedness/gradient responses is not resolved.
- Fold-level D2 ranges are wide (five blocks; PP folds hold 13–31 lakes). Relative importances are unstable when total held-out D2 is small, so the supplementary relative figure shows them only where held-out D2 ≥ 0.02.
- Importance allocations are descriptive Shapley/Owen attributions on held-out blocks; TP, TN and Chl-a are collinear (VIF 2.3–3.3 in US sets).
- Context slopes are conditional averages over actual site configurations and mix TP spread with covarying predictors.
- Failed coalitions: only 2 allocation sets (zooplankton turnover, varying-spread model).
